# Game mechanics

How the engine applies the rules of Ironsworn/Starforged, Mythic GME 2e, the Adventure Crafter, and Blades in the Dark. Where it departs from a source on purpose is listed in `docs/divergences.md`.

## Game master and ruleset

The game master (`gamemaster/`) never reads the ruleset. It asks the questions of `gamemaster/questions.py` → `RulesetAnswers` in its own words, the ruleset answers them in `ruleset/answers.py` from its yaml (`ruleset/answers.yaml`), and the game package installs those answers when it loads (`game/__init__.py`). How an action went is an outcome, success, partial, or failure (`result_outcomes`), beside the engine's own labels for a turn without a roll (`outcomes.unrolled` in `gamemaster/outcomes.yaml`: dialog, oracle, opening), and `gamemaster/questions.py` → `outcome_of` turns a result the session log holds into one. Code outside the ruleset (the game package, prompt assembly, the Director, and the server) decides on a roll only by its outcome (`gained` when the outcome is one of `outcomes.gained`) and otherwise passes the ruleset's result on as it is: to the ruleset, to the narrator as the type of its `<result>` tag, whose text and narrative direction the outcome chooses, and to the player in a momentum burn's offer, under its label in `strings/result.yaml`. What kind of action it was is a move category (`ruleset/move_categories.yaml`). How close an NPC is to the character is a number from 0 to 10, a connection's filled boxes in Ironsworn, which the game master bands by its own thresholds (the stance, the information gate, position and effect, keyed scenes). How hard pressed the character is comes as a level per meter, critical, low, or steady (`strain`); whether the previous action gained an advantage from `advantage_moves`; the bonus an action itself adds to its effect from `effect_bonus_moves`; how long it takes from `ruleset/time_progression_map.yaml`, where a change of place takes `location.change_time`; how it reads in a memory from `action_phrases`; and how far each danger has advanced, a threat's filled menace boxes in Ironsworn. The game master also tells the ruleset what happens on its side: it asks for a new danger (Threats and clocks), says that two NPCs were one person, so the ruleset merges their connections, and says that a clock filled, so a filled progress clock completes the progress track of its name. A value the turn already hands along, such as the outcome that moves the chaos factor or settles the facts, comes as a parameter in the game master's words.

## Moves and outcomes

Move definitions (stats, roll types, trigger conditions) load from each setting's Datasworn JSON (`ruleset/moves.py` → `get_moves`). The engine's own moves, `dialog`, `ask_the_oracle`, and `world_shaping`, live in `ruleset/engine_moves.yaml`, the single source read by the available-moves filter, the Brain schema, and the narrator. `tools/builtins.py` → `available_moves` filters the moves by game state with the rules in `ruleset/move_availability.yaml`, and the Brain chooses only from that list: a move that rolls or marks a track is offered only while a track of its type is active (`active_track`), Enter the Fray and Battle only outside a fight (`no_active_track`), and the moves of a fight also by the combat position (`combat_pos_in`); a rule naming a track type that `track_types_by_category` does not know stops loading.

The action roll is `ruleset/rolls.py` → `roll_action`: one d6 plus the stat, at most 10, against two d10; `roll_progress` rolls a progress track. A move whose Datasworn roll options name no stat rolls with what they name instead, whatever stat the Brain gives: a condition meter such as supply for Check Your Gear, Set a Course, and Make Camp, or the rank of the targeted connection for Develop Your Relationship (`ruleset/roll_sources.py` → `roll_source_without_stat`). A move's Datasworn conditions are its ways to roll, each with the book's words for when it applies, and the Brain sees them that way (`ai/brain.py` → `_ways`). A way whose method picks among its stats, as Heal's "Mend your own wounds" rolls the lower of iron and wits, is answered with the method's name, and the engine rolls with the stat it picks (`ruleset/roll_sources.py` → `roll_by_method`); a roll the engine makes itself, as for a follow-up move, takes what each way's method picks and, where the player would choose, the highest (`best_roll_option`). Each layer keeps the dice it rolls in its own `dice.yaml`: the action and challenge dice, the score cap, and the result that each number of challenge dice beaten gives (`results_by_dice_beaten`, which a burn of momentum and a downgrade in a bad spot also read) in `ruleset/dice.yaml`, Mythic's in `gamemaster/dice.yaml`, and the Adventure Crafter's in `crafters/dice.yaml`; the ticks per box of a track are in `ruleset/progress.yaml`. `ruleset/move_outcome.py` → `resolve_move_outcome` reads a list of effects per move and result from `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`. Simple effects (momentum, resources, progress, combat position in `WorldState.combat_position`) are pure data, so adding a simple move is one yaml entry, plus the category of its track in `ruleset/track_moves.yaml` when it marks progress (Progress tracks below). Where a move's weak hit costs a complication rather than a resource, as the weak hits of Gather Information (a complication or a new danger) and Compel (a demand in return) do, the outcome carries a `complication` effect, and the narrator receives that complication as a consequence it must tell, worded from the move text in `engine/consequence_templates.yaml`; it may also start a complication clock (Threats and clocks). A result that changes nothing has an empty list. Complex outcomes (suffer, threshold, recovery) use named handlers in `ruleset/move_handlers.py`; the suffer and threshold handlers serve Endure Harm, Endure Stress, Face Death, and Face Desolation, which the engine makes as follow-up moves (Harm, stress, and death below), and the companion moves and Withstand Damage, which no turn reaches yet (roadmap step 19). `ruleset/move_effects.py` parses and dispatches the effects, Pay the Price included (`ruleset/pay_the_price.yaml`). Classic's own outcomes (`move_outcome_overrides`) hold in every setting built on classic, so Delve endures harm and faces death as classic does (`ruleset/outcome_result.py` → `move_outcome_config`). A move rolls its own Datasworn tables (`ruleset/moves.py` → `Move.tables`), keyed by their Datasworn names, whether the move names them by id or carries them inside it as Sundered Isles' replaced moves do: Pay the Price and its story complication, the tables of Endure Harm and Endure Stress, the oracle moves Make a Discovery, Confront Chaos, and Reveal a Danger (`oracle_moves`), and a `table` effect, which rolls Story Clue on a hit on Starforged's Gather Information and Take Decisive Action's table on its weak hit, whose cost row costs a suffer move (`move_table_rows` in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`, `ruleset/move_effects.py` → `roll_move_table`). A miss on Delve's Escape the Depths reveals a danger, and the danger rerolls the rows that send to a site's theme or domain card while no site exists. An oracle result rolled for the narrator (Pay the Price, a story complication, a row of Endure Harm or Endure Stress) reaches it under its table's Datasworn name (`ruleset/move_effects.py` → `roll_oracle_result`), and classic's row that says to roll again and make it worse, which Datasworn leaves as text, is matched by `worse_reroll_prefixes` in `ruleset/pay_the_price.yaml` and told as the next result made worse; and the narrator also receives the move's own text for the result as `<move_outcome>` (`ruleset/moves.py` → `outcome_text`), whose choices and numbers the engine has already applied. Every effect records what it did as a structured consequence (`models_base.py` → `Consequence`): a kind, which is the key of its label in `consequence_labels` in `engine/ai_text.yaml`, and the values the label is filled with, such as the meter and the amount or the NPC's name; the label is what the narrator's result, the metadata call, and the engine's memories read. `ruleset/consequences.py` → `generate_consequence_sentences` picks the sentence for the narrator from `engine/consequence_templates.yaml` by the kind and its values, never by reading the label back: a loss of health or spirit is light or heavy by `meters.heavy_loss_amount` in `ruleset/meters.yaml`, a bond's progress and a disposition shift are told about their NPC, a marked or cleared impact by its label, and a kind with no sentence of its own (progress, experience, a legacy reward, a follow-up move, an oracle row, a companion's meter) reaches the narrator only as its label.

**Datasworn mechanic naming.** Datasworn is the spec for mechanical behaviour, not for the shape of the Python. A Datasworn move becomes a formal move in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml` when it is a player choice the Brain can classify, with a structured outcome. A `no_roll` or `special_track` move that fires by itself when a mechanical condition becomes true becomes an engine trigger with a name that says what the code does, such as `advance_menace_on_miss`, and reaches the mechanic directly instead of going through the move-outcome pipeline.

**Shared outcome resolution.** Three paths produce action narration: normal turns, corrections of a misread input, and momentum burns, and all three run the turn's own code in `game/turn.py`. Each turn keeps the snapshot taken right after its scene test, with the scene type, as `GameState.last_turn_snapshot`. A correction of a misread input restores it and plays the turn again with the corrected input (`game/turn.py` → `replay_turn`): same scene type, the facts already settled at that place, and the dice already rolled, rescored with the new stat, so a correction is never a new roll. A correction of the state edits it and narrates the turn again with what the turn told the narrator, its random events, threat events, filled clocks, and NPC agency, which the snapshot keeps (`TurnSnapshot.random_events`, `threat_events`, `clock_fill_results`, `npc_agency`), so nothing is drawn or ticked a second time; the analysis names NPCs by their id from the engine's list, and an operation the engine cannot apply (no NPC named, an empty name, a time of day that does not exist) is skipped with a warning. A split that names no second figure rolls its name like any new NPC. A new chapter or a succession leaves nothing to correct, since its opening is not a turn. A momentum burn restores a second snapshot, taken right after the roll, resets momentum, and runs `resolve_and_narrate_action` with the better result (`game/momentum_burn.py` → `process_momentum_burn`). Every narration path shares `narrate_scene` and `apply_post_narration`.

**What a miss withholds.** A narrator asked to hold back on a miss still handed out clues, so the engine decides what it has (`prompt_action.py` → `build_action_prompt`). Every NPC present takes `information_gate.miss_stance` (withholding) instead of its matrix stance; the target NPC keeps description and instinct but loses agenda, memories, and secrets, with fact budget 0; bystanders lose their memory hint; the previous Director guidance and a pending revelation wait for a hit. A miss on a `gather_information` move settles the place facts in `fact_resolution.information_miss_settles`, today `useful` no (`gamemaster/facts.py` → `settle_facts_by_miss`). A miss with a match rolls the story-complication oracle instead of Pay the Price where the setting has it (Starforged, Sundered Isles), as Datasworn describes that table. A hit grants in the same way: a target NPC whose stance from the matrix would refuse or deceive (`information_gate.refusing_stances`: a hostile NPC's hostile, stonewalling, confrontational, and misleading) takes `information_gate.hit_stance` instead, yielding, so a hostile NPC compelled or questioned on a hit gives what the roll won and keeps the hostility only in how (`gamemaster/stance_gate.py` → `resolve_npc_stance`); bystanders and turns without a roll keep the matrix's stance.

**Position and effect.** Blades in the Dark's position (controlled, risky, desperate) and effect (limited, standard, great) are computed by the engine for every action roll, before its consequences (`gamemaster/resolvers.py` → `resolve_position`, `resolve_effect`, weights in `gamemaster/position_resolver.yaml` and `gamemaster/effect_resolver.yaml`). Position adds up a score from the strain on the character's meters, the target NPC's disposition and closeness, the chaos factor, a streak of failures or successes, clocks close to full, an advantage the previous turn gained, and a baseline per kind of action; two overrides cap it at risky (an advantage gained while a meter is critical) or raise it to risky (a recovery in crisis). Effect starts from position and adds closeness, the advantage, and the bonus the ruleset gives the action itself (Strike's, in `ruleset/answers.yaml`). Strain, closeness, the advantage, the kind of action, and how earlier actions went are the ruleset's answers (Game master and ruleset below). Position decides how many segments a miss ticks a threat clock (`gamemaster/clocks.yaml` → `miss_ticks_by_position`) and whether a weak hit ticks one: always when desperate, never when controlled, otherwise at the chance `pacing.weak_hit_clock_tick_chance`. Both reach the narrator as a `<position>` tag; effect changes nothing else.

## Progress tracks

`ProgressTrack` holds rank, ticks, and status (active, completed, failed, and bonded for a connection); ticks per mark by rank live in `ruleset/progress.yaml` under `ticks_per_mark`. The background vow becomes a track at creation, and the moves that create tracks are listed in `ruleset/track_moves.yaml` (`track_creating_moves`) and start them after the roll (`ruleset/tracks.py` → `start_track`): a vow, a fight, an expedition, or a journey whatever the result, a connection only on a hit (`tracks_started_on_a_hit`), as Make a Connection says. Undertake an Expedition and classic's Undertake a Journey, which both start a track and mark it, continue the track the Brain names in `target_track` and start one only when it names none. `ruleset/tracks.py` → `find_progress_track` and `complete_track` handle the lifecycle: completing a combat track clears the combat position, and completing a vow resolves its linked threat (`ThreatData.linked_vow_id`). A progress roll completes its track on a hit, strong or weak, as the moves have it (`game/action_resolution.py` → `_apply_track_completion`), except a connection, which Forge a Bond's hit bonds instead (`tracks_bonded_on_a_hit` in `ruleset/track_moves.yaml`); Forge a Bond's weak hit seals the bond at once and tells the narrator that they ask something more first (the `bond_request` effect). A bonded connection stays, as Starforged has it: it keeps its progress, so the NPC's bond holds, it aids with `bonded_add` instead of `add` (`ruleset/roll_bonuses.yaml`, the book's Bolster their influence), Make a Connection does not make it again, and `/tracks` names it (`ruleset/bond.py` → `connection_track` finds an active or bonded one). Develop Your Relationship follows its two branches: with a bond it rolls +the connection's rank for the bonds legacy track, and the Brain is offered it only while a bonded connection exists (`bonded_track` in `ruleset/move_availability.yaml`) and is refused it for an NPC without a bond, since `ruleset/roll_sources.py` → `roll_source_without_stat` rolls a rank only for a bonded connection; without a bond it marks progress per the connection's rank without a roll (`without_bond` in its entry in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`), which the engine does when Test Your Relationship's hit makes it as a follow-up (`game/finalization.py` → `_chain_without_bond`). On a miss a fight is lost (End the Fight, Take Decisive Action), while Fulfill Your Vow, Forge a Bond, Finish an Expedition, and Reach Your Destination recommit: the track keeps one box in classic and loses as many boxes as the lower challenge die in Starforged, and its rank rises by one below epic (`recommit_on_miss` in the move's entry in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`, `ruleset/tracks.py` → `recommit_track`). Take Decisive Action in a bad spot counts a strong hit without a match as a weak hit and a weak hit as a miss (`downgrade_in`, `ruleset/rolls.py` → `downgrade_in_position`). A fight is its combat track: Enter the Fray sets who is in control on every result, completing the track ends the fight, and a successor starts outside it. A move's Datasworn track category maps to a track type through `ruleset/track_moves.yaml` → `track_types_by_category`, and where several tracks of that type are active, the Brain names the one the move is about by its track id (`target_track`); since move availability offers such a move only while its track exists, `find_progress_track` raises when it finds none. Datasworn gives a track category only to progress rolls, so an action move that marks progress (Strike, Clash, Gain Ground, Undertake an Expedition, Undertake a Journey, Delve the Depths, and the scene-challenge moves) names the category of its track in `progress_mark_categories` in the same file, taken from the progress roll of its own move category.

Scene challenges (Starforged, Sundered Isles) are configured but not playable yet: Begin the Scene has no roll and never reaches the Brain, so no scene-challenge track exists, the scene-challenge moves that need one are never offered, and the tension clock their misses should fill does not exist (roadmap step 9k).

Connection tracks carry an NPC's bond: a connection's track has the id of its NPC (`ruleset/bond.py` → `connection_track_id`), so Make a Connection needs the NPC as its target, and `get_npc_bond` reads that track's filled boxes; `NpcData` has no bond field. Two NPCs found to be one person, through a correction's merge or a duplicate absorbed after a rename (`gamemaster/npc/lifecycle.py` → `absorb_npc`), become one: the NPC that stays keeps the connection with the most progress and takes over the other's memories, clocks, and place on the characters list.

## Meters

A character's meters are the ruleset's. Its condition meters come from the Datasworn rules along the setting chain (`rules.condition_meters`, each with its range and starting value; Sundered Isles takes Starforged's, Delve classic's), and momentum from `ruleset/momentum.yaml`. `Resources` holds them by name (`Resources.meters`), with no meter as a field of its own, and `ruleset/meters.py` does their arithmetic: `lose` and `gain` keep a condition meter within its range, `adjust_momentum` keeps momentum between its floor and its max, which every marked impact lowers by one (`max_momentum`), so a gain stops at the lowered max, `reset_momentum` burns it to its reset value less one per impact, and `in_crisis` reads the meters `meters.crisis_at_zero` names in `ruleset/meters.yaml`. A move's effect, a suffer move, or a recovery acts on a meter by its name; a name that is no condition meter of the ruleset, such as a companion's health, changes nothing yet (roadmap step 19). What the narrator and the player are told of a meter is keyed by its name: its descriptions and the flag the narrator gets while it stands at 0 in `engine/narrator_status_descriptions.yaml`, the player's in `status_descriptions.meters` in `engine/status_descriptions.yaml`, and the sentences of a gain or a loss in `engine/consequence_templates.yaml`, where a meter without a sentence reaches the narrator only as its label.

## Impacts, legacy, and assets

Impacts (wounded, shaken, and the rest) are the setting's own, read from its Datasworn rules with their label, the meters they keep from recovering, whether they are permanent, and their description (`ruleset/impacts.py` → `get_impacts`), and applied by `ruleset/impacts.py` → `apply_impact`, `clear_impact`, `blocks_recovery`. The narrator receives each marked impact with its description as `<impact>` in `<character_state>` (`prompt_shared.py` → `_impacts_block`). Every marked impact lowers max momentum by one (Meters above). They are marked by Endure Harm and Endure Stress, by Face Death and Face Desolation, and by supply running out (Harm, stress, and death below); on a hit Heal clears wounded and Starforged's Hearten and Resupply clear shaken and unprepared, classic Sojourn clears the oldest marked condition and takes +1 of its meter (the `clear_condition` effect), and a burden clears when its vow is completed. A marked condition blocks its meter's recovery in Endure's exchange and in the recovery moves' amounts, not in other gains (`docs/divergences.md`).

**Harm, stress, and death.** Every loss of health, spirit, or supply goes through `ruleset/suffer.py` → `suffer`: what the meter cannot take past 0 costs momentum, and supply reaching 0 marks unprepared (`ruleset/suffer.yaml` → `exhausted_impacts`). Lost health or spirit is then endured: in classic Ironsworn every time, as its Endure Harm and Endure Stress begin with the loss, and in Starforged and Sundered Isles when the meter stands at 0 (`endure_always` in the moves' params in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`). Enduring is a follow-up move: `suffer` lists it in `OutcomeResult.follow_up_moves`, and `ruleset/move_outcome.py` → `make_follow_up_moves` rolls it with the higher of its stat and its meter and resolves it through the suffer handler, after the turn's move, its chained move, its boasts, and any full menace track. A miss at 0 marks the first unmarked impact the move offers; when both are marked, the handler rolls the move's Datasworn table, tells its row, and acts on the rows `ruleset/suffer.yaml` → `table_rows` names: mortal harm and being overwhelmed make Face Death or Face Desolation, unconsciousness makes Face Death while a combat track is open, giving up forsakes the newest active vow (`ruleset/tracks.py` → `forsake_vow`), and dying starts a two-segment clock of type `dying`, which fills once per turn, action or dialog, after the turn's move, ends when health is above 0, and makes Face Death when full (`suffer.py` → `tick_dying_clock`). Face Death and Face Desolation roll +heart through the threshold handler: a strong hit returns, a miss ends the game, and a weak hit makes Swear an Iron Vow, whose outcome applies; in classic a miss on that vow ends the game too, and otherwise the character returns bound to an extreme vow (`start_bound_vow`) and marks its burden (cursed or tormented in classic, doomed or tormented in Starforged), which clears when the vow is completed. Health and spirit at 0 bring only the crisis status (`game/finalization.py` → `update_crisis`). In a dialog turn the dying clock, a full menace track, and their follow-up moves resolve before the narrator writes, and the narrator receives their labels as `<consequence>` tags and the status flags the action prompt has. The fixed choices this takes are in `docs/divergences.md`.

A ruleset's legacy tracks are the special tracks its Datasworn rules name and do not call optional, kept by name on the campaign (`CampaignState.legacy`, built by `ruleset/legacy.py` → `new_campaign`): Starforged's quests, bonds, and discoveries, and classic's bonds, which nothing marks yet (roadmap step 9j.2); Delve's optional failure track waits for step 13b, which marks it. Their numbers and experience are configured in `ruleset/legacy.yaml`: `ruleset/legacy.py` → `mark_legacy`, `apply_threat_overcome_bonus`, `advance_asset`. The player spends experience with `/advance new <asset>` or `/advance upgrade <asset>`; `advance_asset` refuses an asset the setting lacks, a new asset already held, an upgrade of an asset not held or with every ability enabled, and an advance the experience does not cover, and the player hears which from `strings/advance.yaml`. Classic Ironsworn earns experience by its own rule: Fulfill Your Vow marks experience by the vow's rank, one rank lower on a weak hit (`experience_by_rank` in `ruleset/legacy.yaml`, applied by `game/finalization.py` → `apply_progress_and_legacy`).

**Boasts.** Draw the Circle takes the boasts the player declares, which the Brain reads into its `boasts` field: up to two on a strong hit, one on a weak hit, none on a miss, each for +1 momentum and its cost from `ruleset/boasts.yaml` (`ruleset/boasts.py` → `apply_boasts`).

Assets: `GameState.asset_abilities` records the enabled abilities of every path and asset, starting from the Datasworn defaults; an upgrade enables the next ability in order (`ruleset/assets.py`). `ruleset/bonuses.py` offers every enabled ability whose text grants an add, and every active connection's aid (from `ruleset/roll_bonuses.yaml`), in a `<bonuses>` block of the Brain's prompt. The Brain names at most one by id in `bonus_id` when the action clearly meets its condition; the engine checks the id and takes the number from the rule text, counting momentum on a hit only when the text ties it to the same add. Only adds are modelled (`docs/divergences.md`).

## NPCs

**Memory and activation.** `gamemaster/npc/memory.py` scores memories for importance, retrieves them, and consolidates them; a memory's emotional weight is derived from the kind of action, its outcome, and the disposition through `gamemaster/memory.yaml` (`gamemaster/engine_memories.py` → `derive_memory_emotion`), with keyword boosts from `emotions/*.yaml`. `gamemaster/npc/activation.py` scores NPCs against the scene with TF-IDF and decides which get full context in the prompt. That decides only whom the narrator's prompt holds: an NPC counts as having been in the scene, for its engine memory of the turn, the scene context, the Mythic characters list, and a reported death, when the prompt held it and it is the action's target or the narration names it (`gamemaster/npc/matching.py` → `present_in_narration`). Otherwise an NPC who had left would be remembered into every later scene, and the scene context naming it would activate it again.

**Stance.** The engine computes each NPC's stance from disposition, bond, and move category through `gamemaster/stance_matrix.yaml` (`gamemaster/stance_gate.py` → `resolve_npc_stance`). The narrator receives a behaviour, such as `stance="evasive" constraint="One fact, then silence."`, instead of raw disposition values.

**Information gating.** A gate level from 0 (name and description, a stranger) to 4 (full secrets) decides what of an NPC enters the narrator prompt (`gamemaster/stance_gate.py` → `compute_npc_gate`, levels in `gamemaster/information_gate.yaml`, filtering in `prompt_shared.py` → `_npc_block`). It rises with scenes known, successful information gathering, and bond, and the stance caps it, so a hostile NPC stays opaque whatever the bond. The narrator cannot reveal what it does not have. The gate also sets how many new facts an NPC may volunteer in a reply.

**Names, lifecycle, agency.** New NPCs get oracle-rolled names from the setting's npc generator (Generators below), and `NpcData.name_rolled` records it. A full name the extraction reports for an NPC whose name the engine rolled, sharing no word with it, becomes a new figure; for a figure the narration named, such as an opening NPC or a lore figure, the revealed name replaces the label, which stays as an alias (`gamemaster/npc/processing.py` → `_apply_name_update`). A new figure that matches a known NPC by its description is that NPC seen again: it keeps its name and gains the new label as an alias, and so does a figure named by whole words of a known NPC's name, such as a first name for a full name, however short the word (`gamemaster/npc/matching.py`). An NPC whose name the engine rolled is matched by its own name and by its earlier labels only: a figure that merely shares a word with such a label, such as "Second deckhand" beside "Lunging deckhand", is a new figure, and no label ever replaces the rolled name. The extraction can name only known NPCs by id, since its schema offers no other, and a chapter or succession opening registers no NPC that the name matcher finds among the known ones (`game/setup_common.py` → `register_extracted_npcs`). `gamemaster/npc/processing.py` applies the extracted metadata, `gamemaster/npc/matching.py` matches names, and `gamemaster/npc/lifecycle.py` merges identities, retires, and reactivates NPCs. `gamemaster/clock_ticks.py` → `check_npc_agency` tells the narrator every `pacing.npc_agency_interval` scenes that an NPC pursues its agenda, and the narrator writes what the NPC does. Agendas, instincts, and arcs come from the Director's reflections (`docs/ai.md`).

## Scenes, chaos, and random events

**Scene structure.** Every turn starts with a scene test, before the Brain call, in the priority keyed > interrupt > altered > expected (`gamemaster/scene.py` → `check_scene`). The keyed branch runs first: if a `KeyedScene` on `narrative.keyed_scenes` has its trigger fire, the highest-priority match consumes itself and sets the scene type `keyed`, and its hint reaches the narrator as `<keyed_scene>`. Otherwise a d10 against the chaos factor decides: expected above it, altered at or below it on an odd roll, interrupt on an even one. Altered scenes roll on the Scene Adjustment Table; interrupt scenes generate a random event. The chaos factor moves per turn (`gamemaster/chaos.yaml`). Pacing is computed from the recent scene intensity (`gamemaster/world.py` → `get_pacing_hint`), not asked of the AI.

**Keyed scenes** are the engine's pre-scheduled beats. Trigger types are registered in `gamemaster/keyed_scenes.yaml` and evaluated through `gamemaster/keyed_scenes.py` → `_EVALUATORS`: `clock_fills`, `threat_menace_phase`, `bond_threshold`, `chaos_extreme`, `scene_count`. `gamemaster/keyed_scenes.py` → `new_keyed_scene`, through which every source makes a keyed scene, validates its trigger type, and for the three pattern triggers (`bond_threshold`, `threat_menace_phase`, `clock_fills`) its value, against the grammar in `pattern_grammars`: `<entity_id>:N`, `any:N`, and for bonds `disposition_<value>:N`, each with a matcher strategy (`highest_bond`, `highest_menace`, `highest_filled`). When a pattern fires, `evaluate_keyed_scenes` binds a concrete entity in `KeyedScene.bound_entity_id` and keeps it from its siblings. Three sources spawn keyed scenes: the Adventure Crafter at chapter start (its `keyed_scene_mapping` maps 33 plot points to triggers, with at most one per plot point per chapter and a cap of `max_keyed_scenes_per_chapter`), random events when they fire (from the event's target), and clock creation (per clock type fractions from `gamemaster/clock_keyed_scenes.yaml`, through `gamemaster/keyed_scenes.py` → `spawn_keyed_scenes_for_clock`).

**Random events.** A four-step pipeline in `gamemaster/random_events.py`: event focus (a d100 over the focus categories, `roll_event_focus`), a target from the weighted Mythic lists, a meaning-table roll on actions or descriptions (`roll_meaning_table`), and a structured `RandomEvent` (`generate_random_event`). Events fire on interrupt scenes and on fate doublets. They reach the narrator as `<random_event>` and `<interrupt_scene>` tags; an interrupt scene's own event appears only as `<interrupt_scene>`.

**Mythic lists.** The threads list starts with the background vow; the characters list starts with the vow's subject, if any, and the opening scene's NPCs. Both live on `NarrativeState`. Present NPCs gain weight. A thread gains one weight a scene when the scene invokes it, which the engine reads from what it can see (`game/scene_finalization.py` → `invoked_threads`): a random event aimed at the thread, a rolled move that acts on its vow (today Fulfill Your Vow; Reach a Milestone joins it once it is a formal move), and a threat linked to its vow that the scene advances or a random event aims at. New NPCs join the characters list as the NPC the engine named, and the lists consolidate past their cap (`gamemaster/random_events.py` → `add_thread_weight`, `add_character_weight`, `consolidate_threads`).

## Fate

Yes/no questions about the fiction, rolled by the engine with odds the engine sets (`gamemaster/fate.py`); the player never picks the odds, and a player's own question reaches fate only as a fact (see Ask the Oracle under Facts). Two methods: the fate chart (a 9×9 odds and chaos matrix, d100) and the fate check (2d10 plus modifiers), both with four answers (yes, no, exceptional yes, exceptional no); doubles whose digit is at most the chaos factor generate a random event, as Mythic's rule says. `fate.default_method` in `gamemaster/fate.yaml` picks the method, and the chaos factor enters only as the chart's column or the check's modifier, as Mythic intends. Both methods read Mythic's own tables in `data/mythic_gme_2e.json`: the chart's cells, and the check's odds and chaos modifiers and answer ranges. `gamemaster/fate.py` → `score_to_odds` turns a score into one of the nine odds through `fate.likelihood_rules.score_to_odds`, the single odds scale.

## Facts

A fact is something the fiction depends on that the game state does not hold, such as whether a door is locked or whether a guard is alert. The fact types live in `gamemaster/fact_resolution.yaml`: each names its subject (`place`, the current location, which the Brain writes as `here`, or `npc`, an NPC id from the Brain's list), a description that the Brain and the narrator both read, a base score, the game-state inputs that shift it with their weight, and whether a hit on an action that depended on the fact clears it. Today there are five: `locked`, `useful`, and `occupied` about the place, `present` and `alert` about an NPC, where `alert` weighs the NPC's disposition from `fate.likelihood_rules.disposition_scores` negatively, so a hostile guard is likely on guard and a friendly one unlikely. A `present` fact answered no about the NPC the action targets releases that target for the turn (`no_releases_target`, `gamemaster/facts.py` → `release_absent_target`), so the scene never holds the NPC both out of reach and as the one the action is aimed at.

The Brain names the undetermined facts an action or question turns on, at most `max_per_turn`, from the types and subjects its schema offers (`ai/schemas.py` → `get_brain_output_schema`). After the Brain's move to another place is applied, `gamemaster/generation.py` → `resolve_turn_facts` sends each through `generate_fact`, behind the categories registered in `gamemaster/generation.yaml`. `gamemaster/facts.py` → `resolve_fact` reuses a fact already settled at this place, or scores the fact type, converts the score to odds, asks `resolve_fate`, and stores the answer with the place it belongs to in `WorldState.facts`. The narrator receives the turn's facts as a `<facts>` block (`prompt_shared.py` → `_facts_block`, templates in `prompts/blocks.yaml`).

A fact belongs to the place where it was resolved. `gamemaster/world.py` → `clear_facts` empties the list when the player moves, when a correction changes the location, at a chapter start, and at a succession. After a roll, `gamemaster/facts.py` → `clear_facts_settled_by_hit` removes the facts of this turn that were answered yes and whose type says a hit settles them, so a door the player just forced is not reported locked again; the narrator still hears the fact this turn. A no stays after a hit, so searching the same place again does not roll the fact again, with one exception: a fact type that a miss on a search settles (`fact_resolution.information_miss_settles`, today `useful` no) is overruled by a hit on an action that turned on it (`overrule_facts_by_hit`). The narrator is then not told that nothing useful is here while the result says the search succeeds, and the place no longer holds that no. A momentum burn keeps the turn's facts and clears them by the better result; a correction of a misread input brings back the turn's facts of the same place (`remember_facts`) instead of rolling them again.

**Ask the Oracle.** The engine move `ask_the_oracle` is the player's question about the fiction. A yes/no question that a fact type covers is answered by that fact, and the oracle table is not rolled; the narrator gets the `task_oracle_facts` task. A question for which the Brain names no fact, an open question or a yes/no question that no fact type covers, rolls the setting's action and theme oracle (`ruleset/tracks.py` → `roll_oracle_answer`) and reaches the narrator as `<oracle_answer>`.

## Generators

An entity category rolls the setting's own tables (`gamemaster/generation.py` → `generate_entity`). The categories are registered in `gamemaster/generation.yaml`, and each setting names under `generators` the table that every role of a category rolls (`docs/settings.md`). The roller follows Datasworn's own instructions on a row (`setting/oracle_rolls.py` → `roll_generator_tables`): an automatic roll replaces its link with what it rolled (an Action and Theme pair), a row that rolls its own table again rolls it as often as it says and treats a repeated row as its `duplicates` rule says, rolled again, kept, or made worse, as in classic's Pay the Price (`setting/oracle_rolls.py` → `roll_rows`, which Pay the Price, the chained oracle moves, and the names of complication clocks roll through as well), and a role's path may name an earlier role, which stands for the table that role's row points to or for its text, so Starforged's location theme picks the feature table of that theme and Sundered Isles' settlement location picks its focus table. A category that the active setting chain does not name raises.

Three categories exist. `npc` rolls the name of a new NPC when the metadata extraction registers one (`gamemaster/npc/naming.py` → `roll_oracle_name`): given and family name, or in Starforged the callsign at `naming.callsign_probability`. `location` and `settlement` are places. When the Brain moves the character to a place that is neither a generated place nor in the location history, it names the place's kind from `generation.place_categories`, and `generate_place_on_arrival` rolls that category and keeps it as a `PlaceRecord` in `WorldState.places`: a theme and a feature in Starforged, a descriptor and a kind of place in classic Ironsworn, a region and a landmark in Sundered Isles, and for a settlement its setting's settlement tables (in classic only its trouble). While the character is at a generated place, every narrator prompt carries its rolls as a `<generated>` block (`prompt_shared.py` → `_generated_place_block`), and a return finds the same place. The name stays the Brain's. Places stay with the world across chapters and successions, and a correction of a misread input that goes to the same place keeps the place it generated. The rolled text already uses the setting's words, and the vocabulary instructions cover it like any other text, so no word is replaced in place. Nothing weights the tables by the world state yet (roadmap step 10).

## Threats and clocks

**Threats.** A threat has a menace track (`ruleset/threats.yaml`). `ruleset/threats.py` → `advance_menace_on_miss` advances the menace of a vow's linked threat on a miss, `tick_autonomous_threats` ticks threats on their own, and `resolve_full_menace` handles a full track through `ruleset/tracks.py` → `forsake_vow`; all three are engine rules that depart from Delve's Advance a Threat and from Forsake Your Vow (`docs/divergences.md`). A full track is handled while a turn resolves, action or dialog, before the narrator writes, so the narrator tells the forsaken vow; a track that its own tick fills at scene end waits for the next turn. Every advance reaches the narrator as `<threat_advance>`: one on a miss in the same turn, one a random event aims at before that turn's roll, and an autonomous tick, which comes after narration, in the next turn, marked as off screen (`WorldState.pending_threat_events`, `take_pending_threat_events`). Where the setting has a table for the threat's category (Delve's nine, under `threats.category_oracle_collection`), the advance carries a roll on it as what the threat does.

Threats also spawn mid-game from two sources, which ask the ruleset for a new danger (Game master and ruleset below). A random event whose focus is listed in `gamemaster/random_events.yaml` → `danger_foci` (currently `pc_negative` and `npc_negative`) names the danger after the event's Mythic action and subject (`gamemaster/random_events.py` → `spawn_danger_from_random_event`). An Adventure Crafter plot point listed in `crafters/adventure_crafter.yaml` → `danger_plot_points` (A New Enemy, Hidden Threat, Enemies, Hunted, A Problem Returns) leaves the name to the ruleset, which rolls a Datasworn cascade on the setting's `oracle_paths.threats` (`ruleset/threats.py` → `add_threat`). The cascade (`setting/cascade.py` → `roll_oracle_cascade`) rolls each table with its rows' own roll instructions (a "Roll twice" row gives both results) and follows the first markdown-link id onward: Delve's `threat/category` leads into nine sub-tables, and the one it reaches is the threat's category, while Starforged, classic Ironsworn, and Sundered Isles take the category that `threats.by_origin` in `ruleset/threats.yaml` gives the plot point, beside the rank of every origin. The ruleset dedupes on `ThreatData.creation_source` and caps the threats both sources spawn at `threats.max_per_chapter`. Mid-game threats have no linked vow, so they do not advance on a miss, but they tick on their own and can fire `threat_menace_phase` keyed scenes.

**Clocks.** A new game starts with one threat clock named after the background vow, sized and filled from `gamemaster/opening.yaml`, and every opening (new game, chapter, succession) starts at the time of day that file names; the AI's opening extraction sets neither. Later clocks spawn the same way as threats. A random event whose focus is in `gamemaster/random_events.yaml` → `clock_creation_mapping` (currently `pc_negative` and `move_away_from_thread` to a threat clock, `move_toward_thread` to a progress clock) names the clock after its Mythic pair; an Adventure Crafter plot point in `crafters/adventure_crafter.yaml` → `clock_creation_mapping` (Time Limit, A Crucial Life Support System Begins To Fail, A Needed Resource Runs Out, Impending Doom) names it after the plot point. Size and owner come from `gamemaster/clocks.yaml` (`default_segments`, `default_owner_kind`), these spawners and the complication clocks below share `max_clocks_per_chapter`, and every new clock from these two spawners gets its keyed scenes at once. The spawn-source prefixes are constants in `gamemaster/spawn_sources.py`.

**Complication clocks.** The user's idea for a complication that begins now and strikes only if the player leaves it, after Blades in the Dark, which turns a complication into a clock (`gamemaster/complications.py`). A weak hit with a `complication` effect starts a world-owned threat clock of `clocks.complication.segments` (4), named by a roll on `name_oracle_path` (the story-complication table where the setting has it) or else by a Mythic action pair, unless `max_open` (1) complication clocks are open or the chapter's clock cap is reached; the narrator gets the danger as a consequence to show as a first sign. It fills one segment at every scene end, the scene that started it included (`tick_chance` 1.0 in `gamemaster/clock_ticks.py` → `tick_autonomous_clocks`), and a miss's tick can reach it like any threat clock, so it strikes within four scenes through the threat fill tag. It has no keyed scenes, so the tag that the danger has arrived is what the narrator tells. The Brain sees open complication clocks as `<dangers>` and names one in `target_clock` when the action works to stop, undo, or escape it; a hit on that action removes the clock and tells the narrator it was headed off (`game/action_resolution.py` → `_maybe_avert_complication`).

**A filled clock** makes `gamemaster/clock_consequences.py` → `resolve_clock_fill` emit a `<clock_filled>` tag from the per-type templates in `gamemaster/clocks.yaml` → `fill_consequences` (`threat`, `scheme`, `progress`), unless a keyed scene from that clock is still pending, which then takes precedence. A filled progress clock also completes the progress track of the same name. Filled threat and scheme clocks do not advance menace. Fills from ticks on a miss and from NPC agency reach the same turn's narrator prompt; fills from the autonomous ticks after narration queue on `WorldState.pending_clock_fills` and open the next turn's prompt.

## Story structure

**Blueprint.** At the start of a chapter `crafters/adventure_crafter.py` → `assemble_blueprint_seed_from_ac` rolls turning points into a frozen `BlueprintSeed`, and `ai/blueprint_voicing.py` → `call_blueprint_voicing` turns it into a setting-specific `StoryBlueprint` in one structured call whose schema fixes the number of acts, revelations, and possible endings. A Kishōtenketsu blueprint skips the turning points and takes its four acts from yaml (`assemble_blueprint_seed_kishotenketsu`). Which of the two structures a chapter gets follows the campaign's tone (`gamemaster/world.py` → `choose_story_structure`): `story.kishotenketsu_probability` gives each tone its chance, from 0.15 for a dark or epic tone to 0.7 for a cozy one. The player picks the tone at character creation, preset to the setting's `default_tone`, and the narrator receives it as its tone authority. Each act's turning point reaches the voicing with its beats, its plotline, and whether it opens, advances, or concludes that plotline, as the Adventure Crafter rolled it (`TurningPoint.plotline`, `opens_plotline`, `concludes_plotline`). After each turning point, `spawn_keyed_scenes_for_turning_point` adds its keyed scenes. The Adventure Crafter is not consulted in the middle of a chapter; pacing there runs on random events, the pacing hint, clocks, threats, the scene structure, and the Director's reflections.

Act transitions are engine-computed at the end of every turn, whether or not the Director runs: an act ends at the last scene of its range (`story_state.py` → `check_act_transition`, called in the scene-end bookkeeping), so the Director after that turn and the next turn's prompts already see the next act (`get_current_act`); `check_story_completion` then decides whether the story is complete.

**Adventure Crafter tables.** The primitives in `crafters/adventure_crafter.py`, with engine config in `crafters/adventure_crafter.yaml` and data in `data/adventure_crafter.json`: five themes from a d10 table, plot points looked up by theme and roll (the JSON covers the themes sparsely), the special ranges Conclusion (1 to 8), None (9 to 24), and Meta (96 to 100), and a dispatch table for the seven meta plot points. The yaml `theme_die_table` is checked against the JSON's `random_themes` on first load. `roll_turning_point` combines two to five plot points: a plotline from `plotlines_list_template` (falling back to `choose_most_logical`), then per plot point a theme by `plot_point_theme_priority` (alternating for the fourth and fifth) and 2d10 for the lookup. A Conclusion flips the active plotline from advancement to conclusion. `NarrativeState` holds the `characters_list`, shared with Mythic's random events and extended with `ac_status`, and the Adventure Crafter's own `plotlines_list`; the meta handlers change both in place, but no turning point calls them yet, so a Meta result has no effect (`docs/divergences.md`); `dispatch_meta` and the Characters List template wait under an orphan-symbol carve-out for roadmap step 9j.7. The character-crafting tables are pure helpers (`roll_character_traits`); their `CharacterTraits` holds one or two identities and descriptors and waits under an orphan-symbol carve-out for roadmap step 11.

## Chapters and succession

**Chapter transitions.** A chapter is what Mythic and the Adventure Crafter call an adventure within a campaign: one blueprint, and a new one when its plotlines are resolved. `game/chapters.py` → `start_new_chapter` closes the old chapter into a `ChapterSummary`, with a record the engine writes from its own data (vows fulfilled, forsaken, and still sworn, plotlines concluded, open threads, people with disposition and bond, the dead, threats with their menace and those overcome, where the chapter ended, opening with the blueprint's central conflict; the title is the chapter's number). What belongs to one chapter starts fresh in `_reset_chapter_mechanics`: the scene count, the chaos factor at Mythic's starting value of 5, the session log and narration history, the blueprint, the time of day, the location history, the facts, and the Director's guidance. Everything else carries over as it stands: progress tracks, threats, impacts, assets, threads, the characters and plotlines lists, health, spirit, supply, momentum, and clocks, since Ironsworn and Starforged recover only through moves. Experience and legacy tracks span the whole campaign, and the generated places stay with the world; NPCs and their connection tracks carry over through `game.npcs` and `_prepare_npcs_for_new_chapter`. A game that is over continues through succession, never a new chapter.

**Character succession.** When the protagonist dies (on a miss at Face Death or Face Desolation, or in classic on a miss on the vow sworn there; Harm, stress, and death above) or retires, which the game records as its `end_reason` (death, despair, or retire; the game is over while it is set), the campaign continues with a new protagonist in the same world, in two steps gated by `CampaignState.pending_succession`. `game/succession.py` → `prepare_succession` archives the predecessor into `campaign.predecessors` and locks the inheritance rolls onto that record, which keeps inheritance the same across a reload. `start_succession_with_character` reads those rolls, closes the chapter, carries NPCs and their connection tracks over per `ruleset/succession.yaml`, and keeps every threat and the open threads that were not the character's vows or creation. The rest goes: resources, impacts, assets, and the other progress tracks leave with the character, every clock is cleared, unlike at a chapter start, a fight ends with its combat position, and the scene count, chaos factor, time of day, and facts restart. It then seeds the successor's legacy, replaces the character, writes the opening, and clears the flag. The mechanics live in `ruleset/succession.py` → `build_predecessor_record`, `run_inheritance_rolls`, `seed_successor_legacy`, `apply_npc_carryover`. Unknown roll outcomes and NPC statuses raise.
