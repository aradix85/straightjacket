# Game mechanics

How the engine applies the rules of Ironsworn/Starforged, Mythic GME 2e, the Adventure Crafter, and Blades in the Dark. Where it departs from a source on purpose is listed in `docs/divergences.md`.

## Moves and outcomes

Move definitions (stats, roll types, trigger conditions) load from each setting's Datasworn JSON (`datasworn/moves.py` → `get_moves`). The engine's own moves, `dialog`, `ask_the_oracle`, and `world_shaping`, live in `engine/engine_moves.yaml`, the single source read by the available-moves filter, the Brain schema, and the narrator. `tools/builtins.py` → `available_moves` filters the moves by game state (combat position, active tracks), and the Brain chooses only from that list.

The action roll is `mechanics/consequences.py` → `roll_action`: one d6 plus the stat, at most 10, against two d10; `roll_progress` rolls a progress track. `mechanics/move_outcome.py` → `resolve_move_outcome` reads a list of effects per move and result from `engine/move_outcomes.yaml`. Simple effects (momentum, resources, progress, combat position in `WorldState.combat_position`) are pure data, so adding a simple move is one yaml entry. Complex outcomes (suffer, threshold, recovery) use named handlers in `mechanics/move_handlers.py`, and `mechanics/move_effects.py` parses and dispatches the effects, Pay the Price included (`engine/pay_the_price.yaml`). `mechanics/consequences.py` → `generate_consequence_sentences` turns outcomes into sentences for the narrator from `engine/consequence_templates.yaml`.

**Datasworn mechanic naming.** Datasworn is the spec for mechanical behaviour, not for the shape of the Python. A Datasworn move becomes a formal move in `engine/move_outcomes.yaml` when it is a player choice the Brain can classify, with a structured outcome. A `no_roll` or `special_track` move that fires by itself when a mechanical condition becomes true becomes an engine trigger with a name that says what the code does, such as `advance_menace_on_miss`, and reaches the mechanic directly instead of going through the move-outcome pipeline.

**Shared outcome resolution.** Three paths produce action narration: normal turns, corrections of a misread input, and momentum burns, and all three run the turn's own code in `game/turn.py`. The burn restores the state taken right after the roll, resets momentum, and runs `resolve_and_narrate_action` with the better result. A correction of a misread input restores the state taken right after the scene test and plays the turn again with the corrected input (`replay_turn`): same scene type, the facts already settled at that place, and the dice already rolled, rescored with the new stat, so a correction is never a new roll. Every narration path shares `narrate_scene` and `apply_post_narration`.

## Progress tracks

`ProgressTrack` holds rank, ticks, and status (active, completed, failed); ticks per mark by rank live in `engine/progress.yaml` under `track_types.default.ticks_per_mark`. The background vow becomes a track at creation, and the moves that create tracks are listed in `engine/track_moves.yaml` (`track_creating_moves`, plus `scene_challenge_progress_moves` for scene challenges). `mechanics/tracks.py` → `find_progress_track`, `complete_track`, `sync_combat_tracks` handle the lifecycle: completing a combat track clears the combat position, completing a vow resolves its linked threat (`ThreatData.linked_vow_id`), and orphaned combat tracks are cleaned up.

Connection tracks carry an NPC's bond: `npc/bond.py` → `get_npc_bond` reads the connection track's filled boxes; `NpcData` has no bond field.

## Impacts, legacy, and assets

Impacts (wounded, shaken, and the rest) are configured in `engine/impacts.yaml` and applied by `mechanics/impacts.py` → `apply_impact`, `clear_impact`, `blocks_recovery`, `recalc_max_momentum`.

Legacy tracks (quests, bonds, discoveries) and experience are configured in `engine/legacy.yaml` and kept on the campaign: `mechanics/legacy.py` → `mark_legacy`, `apply_threat_overcome_bonus`, `advance_asset`.

Assets: `GameState.asset_abilities` records the enabled abilities of every path and asset, starting from the Datasworn defaults; an upgrade enables the next ability in order (`mechanics/assets.py`). `mechanics/bonuses.py` offers every enabled ability whose text grants an add, and every active connection's aid (from `engine/roll_bonuses.yaml`), in a `<bonuses>` block of the Brain's prompt. The Brain names at most one by id in `bonus_id` when the action clearly meets its condition; the engine checks the id and takes the number from the rule text, counting momentum on a hit only when the text ties it to the same add. Only adds are modelled; rerolls, other effects, condition meters for companions and vehicles, and choosing which ability an upgrade enables are roadmap step 18.

## NPCs

**Memory and activation.** `npc/memory.py` scores memories for importance, retrieves them, and consolidates them; a memory's emotional weight is derived from the move category, the result, and the disposition through `engine/memory.yaml` (`mechanics/engine_memories.py` → `derive_memory_emotion`), with keyword boosts from `emotions/*.yaml`. `npc/activation.py` scores NPCs against the scene with TF-IDF and decides which get full context in the prompt.

**Stance.** The engine computes each NPC's stance from disposition, bond, and move category through `engine/stance_matrix.yaml` (`mechanics/stance_gate.py` → `resolve_npc_stance`). The narrator receives a behaviour, such as `stance="evasive" constraint="One fact, then silence."`, instead of raw disposition values.

**Information gating.** A gate level from 0 (name and description, a stranger) to 4 (full secrets) decides what of an NPC enters the narrator prompt (`mechanics/stance_gate.py` → `compute_npc_gate`, levels in `engine/information_gate.yaml`, filtering in `prompt_shared.py` → `_npc_block`). It rises with scenes known, successful information gathering, and bond, and the stance caps it, so a hostile NPC stays opaque whatever the bond. The narrator cannot reveal what it does not have. The gate also sets how many new facts an NPC may volunteer in a reply.

**Names, lifecycle, agency.** New NPCs get oracle-rolled names (`npc/naming.py` → `roll_oracle_name`, from the setting's `oracle_paths.names`). `npc/processing.py` applies the extracted metadata, `npc/matching.py` matches names, and `npc/lifecycle.py` merges identities, retires, and reactivates NPCs. `mechanics/consequences.py` → `check_npc_agency` lets NPCs act on their own every `pacing.npc_agency_interval` scenes. Agendas, instincts, and arcs come from the Director's reflections (`docs/ai.md`).

## Scenes, chaos, and random events

**Scene structure.** Every turn starts with a scene test, before the Brain call, in the priority keyed > interrupt > altered > expected (`mechanics/scene.py` → `check_scene`). The keyed branch runs first: if a `KeyedScene` on `narrative.keyed_scenes` has its trigger fire, the highest-priority match consumes itself and sets the scene type `keyed`, and its hint reaches the narrator as `<keyed_scene>`. Otherwise a d10 against the chaos factor decides: expected above it, altered at or below it on an odd roll, interrupt on an even one. Altered scenes roll on the Scene Adjustment Table; interrupt scenes generate a random event. The chaos factor moves per turn (`engine/chaos.yaml`). Pacing is computed from the recent scene intensity (`mechanics/world.py` → `get_pacing_hint`), not asked of the AI.

**Keyed scenes** are the engine's pre-scheduled beats. Trigger types are registered in `engine/keyed_scenes.yaml` and evaluated through `mechanics/keyed_scenes.py` → `_EVALUATORS`: `clock_fills`, `threat_menace_phase`, `bond_threshold`, `chaos_extreme`, `scene_count`. `KeyedScene` validates its trigger type, and for the three pattern triggers (`bond_threshold`, `threat_menace_phase`, `clock_fills`) its value, against the grammar in `pattern_grammars`: `<entity_id>:N`, `any:N`, and for bonds `disposition_<value>:N`, each with a matcher strategy (`highest_bond`, `highest_menace`, `highest_filled`). When a pattern fires, `evaluate_keyed_scenes` binds a concrete entity in `KeyedScene.bound_entity_id` and keeps it from its siblings. Three sources spawn keyed scenes: the Adventure Crafter at chapter start (its `keyed_scene_mapping` maps 33 plot points to triggers, with at most one per plot point per chapter and a cap of `max_keyed_scenes_per_chapter`), random events when they fire (from the event's target), and clock creation (per clock type fractions from `engine/clock_keyed_scenes.yaml`, through `mechanics/keyed_scenes.py` → `spawn_keyed_scenes_for_clock`).

**Random events.** A four-step pipeline in `mechanics/random_events.py`: event focus (a d100 over the focus categories, `roll_event_focus`), a target from the weighted Mythic lists, a meaning-table roll on actions or descriptions (`roll_meaning_table`), and a structured `RandomEvent` (`generate_random_event`). Events fire on interrupt scenes and on fate doublets. They reach the narrator as `<random_event>` and `<interrupt_scene>` tags; an interrupt scene's own event appears only as `<interrupt_scene>`.

**Mythic lists.** The threads list starts with the background vow plus tensions derived from the chosen truths (templates in `engine/creation.yaml`); the characters list starts with the vow's subject, if any, and the opening scene's NPCs. Both live on `NarrativeState`. Present NPCs and threads gain weight, new NPCs join the characters list, and the lists consolidate past their cap (`mechanics/random_events.py` → `add_thread_weight`, `add_character_weight`, `consolidate_threads`).

## Fate

Yes/no questions about the fiction, asked by the engine, never by the player directly (`mechanics/fate.py`). Two methods: the fate chart (a 9×9 odds and chaos matrix, d100) and the fate check (2d10 plus modifiers), both with four answers (yes, no, exceptional yes, exceptional no); a doublet generates a random event. `fate.default_method` in `engine/fate.yaml` picks the method, and the chaos factor enters only as the chart's column or the check's modifier, as Mythic intends. `mechanics/fate.py` → `score_to_odds` turns a score into one of the nine odds through `fate.likelihood_rules.score_to_odds`, the single odds scale.

## Facts

A fact is something the fiction depends on that the game state does not hold, such as whether a door is locked or whether a guard is alert. The fact types live in `engine/fact_resolution.yaml`: each names its subject (`place`, the current location, which the Brain writes as `here`, or `npc`, an NPC id from the Brain's list), a description that the Brain and the narrator both read, a base score, the game-state inputs that shift it with their weight, and whether a hit on an action that depended on the fact clears it. Today there are five: `locked`, `useful`, and `occupied` about the place, `present` and `alert` about an NPC, where `alert` weighs the NPC's disposition from `fate.likelihood_rules.disposition_scores` negatively, so a hostile guard is likely on guard and a friendly one unlikely.

The Brain names the undetermined facts an action or question turns on, at most `max_per_turn`, from the types and subjects its schema offers (`ai/schemas.py` → `get_brain_output_schema`). After the Brain's move to another place is applied, `mechanics/generation.py` → `resolve_turn_facts` sends each through `generate`, the single entry point for engine-generated content, whose categories are registered in `engine/generation.yaml`. `mechanics/facts.py` → `resolve_fact` reuses a fact already settled at this place, or scores the fact type, converts the score to odds, asks `resolve_fate`, and stores the answer with the place it belongs to in `WorldState.facts`. The narrator receives the turn's facts as a `<facts>` block (`prompt_shared.py` → `_facts_block`, templates in `prompts/blocks.yaml`).

A fact belongs to the place where it was resolved. `mechanics/world.py` → `clear_facts` empties the list when the player moves, when a correction changes the location, at a chapter start, and at a succession. After a roll, `mechanics/facts.py` → `clear_facts_settled_by_hit` removes the facts of this turn that were answered yes and whose type says a hit settles them, so a door the player just forced is not reported locked again; the narrator still hears the fact this turn. A no stays after a hit, so searching the same place again does not roll the fact again. A momentum burn keeps the turn's facts and clears them by the better result; a correction of a misread input brings back the turn's facts of the same place (`remember_facts`) instead of rolling them again.

**Ask the Oracle.** The engine move `ask_the_oracle` is the player's question about the fiction. A yes/no question that a fact type covers is answered by that fact, and the oracle table is not rolled; the narrator gets the `task_oracle_facts` task. An open question rolls the setting's action and theme oracle (`mechanics/tracks.py` → `roll_oracle_answer`) and reaches the narrator as `<oracle_answer>`.

## Threats and clocks

**Threats.** A threat has a menace track (`engine/threats.yaml`). `mechanics/threats.py` → `advance_menace_on_miss` advances the menace of a vow's linked threat on a miss, `tick_autonomous_threats` ticks threats on their own, and `resolve_full_menace` handles a full track (Forsake Your Vow).

Threats also spawn mid-game from two sources, each naming them its own way. A random event whose focus is listed in `engine/random_events.yaml` → `threat_creation_mapping` (currently `pc_negative` and `npc_negative`) names the threat after the event's Mythic action and subject (`spawn_threat_from_random_event`). An Adventure Crafter plot point listed in `engine/adventure_crafter.yaml` → `threat_creation_mapping` (A New Enemy, Hidden Threat, Enemies, Hunted, A Problem Returns) names it with a Datasworn cascade roll on the setting's `oracle_paths.threats` (`spawn_threats_for_turning_point`). The cascade (`datasworn/cascade.py` → `roll_oracle_cascade`) follows markdown-link ids in oracle rows: Delve's `threat/category` leads into nine sub-tables, while Starforged, classic Ironsworn, and Sundered Isles roll a single table. Both spawners dedupe on `ThreatData.creation_source` and share `max_threats_per_chapter`. Mid-game threats have no linked vow, so they do not advance on a miss, but they tick on their own and can fire `threat_menace_phase` keyed scenes.

**Clocks.** A new game starts with one threat clock named after the background vow, sized and filled from `engine/opening.yaml`, and every opening (new game, chapter, succession) starts at the time of day that file names; the AI's opening extraction sets neither. Later clocks spawn the same way as threats. A random event whose focus is in `engine/random_events.yaml` → `clock_creation_mapping` (currently `pc_negative` and `move_away_from_thread` to a threat clock, `move_toward_thread` to a progress clock) names the clock after its Mythic pair; an Adventure Crafter plot point in `engine/adventure_crafter.yaml` → `clock_creation_mapping` (Time Limit, A Crucial Life Support System Begins To Fail, A Needed Resource Runs Out, Impending Doom) names it after the plot point. Size and owner come from `engine/clocks.yaml` (`default_segments`, `default_owner_kind`), both spawners share `max_clocks_per_chapter`, and every new clock gets its keyed scenes at once. The spawn-source prefixes are constants in `mechanics/spawn_sources.py`.

**A filled clock** makes `mechanics/clock_consequences.py` → `resolve_clock_fill` emit a `<clock_filled>` tag from the per-type templates in `engine/clocks.yaml` → `fill_consequences` (`threat`, `scheme`, `progress`), unless a keyed scene from that clock is still pending, which then takes precedence. A filled progress clock also completes the progress track of the same name. Filled threat and scheme clocks do not advance menace. Fills from ticks on a miss and from NPC agency reach the same turn's narrator prompt; fills from the autonomous ticks after narration queue on `WorldState.pending_clock_fills` and open the next turn's prompt.

## Story structure

**Blueprint.** At the start of a chapter `mechanics/adventure_crafter.py` → `assemble_blueprint_seed_from_ac` rolls turning points into a frozen `BlueprintSeed`, and `ai/blueprint_voicing.py` → `call_blueprint_voicing` turns it into a setting-specific `StoryBlueprint` in one structured call whose schema fixes the number of acts, revelations, and possible endings. A Kishōtenketsu blueprint skips the turning points and takes its four acts from yaml (`assemble_blueprint_seed_kishotenketsu`). After each turning point, `spawn_keyed_scenes_for_turning_point` adds its keyed scenes. The Adventure Crafter is not consulted in the middle of a chapter; pacing there runs on random events, the pacing hint, clocks, threats, the scene structure, and the Director's reflections.

Act transitions are engine-computed: an act ends when the scene count leaves its range (`director.py` → `_check_engine_act_transition`, with `story_state.py` → `get_current_act`, `check_story_completion`).

**Adventure Crafter tables.** The primitives in `mechanics/adventure_crafter.py`, with engine config in `engine/adventure_crafter.yaml` and data in `data/adventure_crafter.json`: five themes from a d10 table, plot points looked up by theme and roll (the JSON covers the themes sparsely), the special ranges Conclusion (1 to 8), None (9 to 24), and Meta (96 to 100), and a dispatch table for the seven meta plot points. The yaml `theme_die_table` is checked against the JSON's `random_themes` on first load. `roll_turning_point` combines two to five plot points: a plotline from `plotlines_list_template` (falling back to `choose_most_logical`), then per plot point a theme by `plot_point_theme_priority` (alternating for the fourth and fifth) and 2d10 for the lookup. A Conclusion flips the active plotline from advancement to conclusion. `NarrativeState` holds the `characters_list`, shared with Mythic's random events and extended with `ac_status` and `ac_turning_point_count`, and the Adventure Crafter's own `plotlines_list`; the meta handlers change both in place. The character-crafting tables are pure helpers (`roll_character_traits`); their `CharacterTraits` holds one or two identities and descriptors and waits under an orphan-symbol carve-out for roadmap step 11.

## Chapters and succession

**Chapter transitions.** Three functions in `game/chapters.py`: `_close_previous_chapter` builds a `ChapterSummary` with the narrative summary (`ai/chapter_summary.py` → `call_chapter_summary`) and a mechanical snapshot, `_reset_chapter_mechanics` zeros every chapter-spanning field, and `_restore_chapter_mechanics` replays the snapshot. A new chapter-spanning field must be added to all three. Experience and legacy tracks span the whole campaign and stay out of `ChapterSummary`; NPCs and their connection tracks carry over through `game.npcs` and `_prepare_npcs_for_new_chapter`.

**Character succession.** When the protagonist dies (a miss on Face Death, or health and spirit both at zero) or retires, the campaign continues with a new protagonist in the same world, in two steps gated by `CampaignState.pending_succession`. `game/succession.py` → `prepare_succession` archives the predecessor into `campaign.predecessors` and locks the inheritance rolls onto that record, which keeps inheritance the same across a reload. `start_succession_with_character` reads those rolls, closes the chapter, carries NPCs over per `engine/succession.yaml`, clears the character's own state while keeping world-level threats and open threads that were not the character's vows or creation, seeds the successor's legacy, replaces the character, writes the opening, and clears the flag. The mechanics live in `mechanics/succession.py` → `build_predecessor_record`, `run_inheritance_rolls`, `seed_successor_legacy`, `apply_npc_carryover`. Unknown roll outcomes and NPC statuses raise.
