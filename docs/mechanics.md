# Game mechanics

How the engine applies the rules of Ironsworn/Starforged, Mythic GME 2e, the Adventure Crafter, and Blades in the Dark. Where it departs from a source on purpose is listed in `docs/divergences.md`.

## Game master and ruleset

The game master (`gamemaster/`) never reads the ruleset. It asks the questions of `gamemaster/questions.py` → `RulesetAnswers` in its own words, the ruleset answers them in `ruleset/answers.py` from its yaml (`ruleset/answers.yaml`), and the game package installs those answers when it loads (`game/__init__.py`). How an action went is an outcome, success, partial, or failure (`result_outcomes`), beside the engine's own labels for a turn without a roll (`outcomes.unrolled` in `gamemaster/outcomes.yaml`: dialog, oracle, opening), and `gamemaster/questions.py` → `outcome_of` turns a result the session log holds into one. Code outside the ruleset (the game package, prompt assembly, the Director, and the server) decides on a roll only by its outcome (`gained` when the outcome is one of `outcomes.gained`) and otherwise passes the ruleset's result on as it is: to the ruleset, to the narrator as the type of its `<result>` tag, whose text and narrative direction the outcome chooses and which the narrator's rules name by the ruleset's own names (`ruleset/answers.py` → `results_by_outcome`), and to the player in a momentum burn's offer, under its label in `strings/result.yaml`. What kind of action it was is a move category (`ruleset/move_categories.yaml`). How close an NPC is to the character is a number from 0 to 10, a connection's filled boxes in Ironsworn, which the game master bands by its own thresholds (the stance, the information gate, position and effect, keyed scenes). How hard pressed the character is comes as a level per meter, critical, low, or steady (`strain`); whether the previous action gained an advantage from `advantage_moves`; whether an action gathers information, which counts toward the information gate on a hit, from `information_moves`; the bonus an action itself adds to its effect from `effect_bonus_moves`; how long it takes from `ruleset/time_progression_map.yaml`, where a change of place takes `location.change_time` in `gamemaster/location.yaml`; how it reads in a memory from `action_phrases`; and how far each danger has advanced, a threat's filled menace boxes in Ironsworn. The game master also tells the ruleset what happens on its side: it asks for a new danger (Threats and clocks), says that two NPCs were one person, so the ruleset merges their connections, and says that a clock filled, so a filled progress clock completes the progress track of its name. A value the turn already hands along, such as the outcome that moves the chaos factor or settles the facts, comes as a parameter in the game master's words.

## Moves and outcomes

Move definitions (stats, roll types, trigger conditions) load from the ruleset's Datasworn files (`ruleset/moves.py` → `get_moves`; `docs/settings.md`, Rulesets), a later file's move replacing an earlier one's, as Sundered Isles replaces some of Starforged's. Dialog and asking the oracle are the game master's own moves in any system, with no roll, in `gamemaster/moves.yaml` with the time each takes; `gamemaster/moves.py` says whether a turn plays as dialog (`is_dialog_branch`) and whether it asks the oracle. World shaping, an action roll with the ruleset's stats, is the ruleset's own move in `ruleset/engine_moves.yaml`. `ruleset/move_availability.py` → `available_moves` filters the Datasworn moves by game state with the rules in `ruleset/move_availability.yaml`, and the Brain chooses only from those, the game master's moves, and world shaping (`ai/brain.py` → `_offered_moves`): a move that rolls or marks a track is offered only while a track of its type is active (`active_track`), unless the move starts that track, as Undertake an Expedition and Undertake a Journey do; Enter the Fray and Battle only outside a fight (`no_active_track`); and Strike, Clash, Gain Ground, and React Under Fire also by the combat position (`combat_pos_in`). Every Datasworn move whose roll type can be offered (not `unoffered_roll_types`) has an entry there, or `available_moves` raises, and a rule naming a track type that `track_types_by_category` does not know stops loading.

A turn makes its move through `ruleset/make_move.py`: `roll_move` rolls it, a progress roll on its track or an action roll with its stat, bonus, and adds, and `resolve_move` resolves its outcome with any chained move and boasts, so the game package names neither a Datasworn roll type nor a Datasworn move. The action roll is `ruleset/rolls.py` → `roll_action`: one d6 plus the stat, at most 10, against two d10; `roll_progress` rolls a progress track. After a roll the player is offered to burn momentum when the move's Datasworn data allows it (`allow_momentum_burn`, which every action roll does and no progress roll) and momentum would better the result (`ruleset/rolls.py` → `can_burn_momentum`). A move whose Datasworn roll options name no stat rolls with what they name instead, whatever stat the Brain gives: a condition meter such as supply for Check Your Gear, Set a Course, and Make Camp, or the rank of the targeted connection for Develop Your Relationship (`ruleset/roll_sources.py` → `roll_source_without_stat`). A move's Datasworn conditions are its ways to roll, each with the book's words for when it applies, and the Brain sees them that way (`ai/brain.py` → `_ways`). A way whose method picks among its stats, as Heal's "Mend your own wounds" rolls the lower of iron and wits, is answered with the method's name, and the engine rolls with the stat it picks (`ruleset/roll_sources.py` → `roll_by_method`); a roll the engine makes itself, as for a follow-up move, takes what each way's method picks and, where the player would choose, the highest (`best_roll_option`). Each layer keeps the dice it rolls in its own `dice.yaml`: the action and challenge dice, the score cap, and the result that each number of challenge dice beaten gives (`results_by_dice_beaten`, which a burn of momentum and a downgrade in a bad spot also read) in `ruleset/dice.yaml`, Mythic's in `gamemaster/dice.yaml`, and the Adventure Crafter's in `crafters/dice.yaml`; the ticks per box of a track are in `ruleset/progress.yaml`. `ruleset/move_outcome.py` → `resolve_move_outcome` reads a list of effects per move and result from `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`. Simple effects (momentum, resources, progress, combat position in `WorldState.combat_position`) are pure data, so adding a simple move is yaml only: its outcomes there, its category in `ruleset/move_categories.yaml`, its availability, and the category of its track in `ruleset/track_moves.yaml` when it marks progress (Progress tracks below). Where a move's weak hit costs a complication rather than a resource, as the weak hits of Gather Information (a complication or a new danger) and Compel (a demand in return) do, the outcome carries a `complication` effect, and the narrator receives that complication as a consequence it must tell, worded from the move text in `engine/consequence_templates.yaml`; it may also start a complication clock (Threats and clocks). A result that changes nothing has an empty list. Complex outcomes (suffer, threshold, recovery) use named handlers in `ruleset/move_handlers.py`; the suffer and threshold handlers serve Endure Harm, Endure Stress, Face Death, and Face Desolation, which the engine makes as follow-up moves (Harm, stress, and death below), and the companion moves and Withstand Damage, which no turn reaches yet (roadmap step 19). `ruleset/move_effects.py` parses and dispatches the effects, Pay the Price included (`ruleset/pay_the_price.yaml`). Classic's own outcomes are the classic ruleset's (`move_outcome_overrides`, keyed by ruleset), so Delve, which plays classic, endures harm and faces death as classic does (`ruleset/outcome_result.py` → `move_outcome_config`). A move rolls the tables Datasworn carries inside it (`ruleset/moves.py` → `Move.tables`), keyed by their Datasworn names: Pay the Price, the tables of Endure Harm and Endure Stress, the oracle moves Make a Discovery, Confront Chaos, and Reveal a Danger (`oracle_moves`), and Take Decisive Action's table. It also offers the tables it suggests, where the setting chain holds them (`Move.suggestions`, `ruleset/moves.py` → `move_table`, `offers_table`): Starforged's Pay the Price suggests Story Complication and its Gather Information Story Clue, which Sundered Isles takes from Starforged. A `table` effect rolls one of them: Story Clue on a hit on Gather Information, and Take Decisive Action's table on its weak hit, whose cost row costs a suffer move (`move_table_rows` in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`, `ruleset/move_effects.py` → `roll_move_table`). A miss on Delve's Escape the Depths reveals a danger, and the danger rerolls the rows that send to a site's theme or domain card, since no site exists (`reroll_prefixes`). An oracle result rolled for the narrator (Pay the Price, a story complication, a row of Endure Harm or Endure Stress) reaches it under its table's Datasworn name (`ruleset/move_effects.py` → `roll_oracle_result`), and classic's row that says to roll again and make it worse, which Datasworn leaves as text, is matched by `worse_reroll_prefixes` in `ruleset/pay_the_price.yaml` and told as the next result made worse; and the narrator also receives the move's own text for the result as `<move_outcome>` (`ruleset/moves.py` → `outcome_text`), whose choices and numbers the engine has already applied. Every effect but the combat position records what it did as a structured consequence (`models_base.py` → `Consequence`): a kind, which is the key of its label in `consequence_labels` in `engine/ai_text.yaml`, and the values the label is filled with, such as the meter and the amount or the NPC's name; the label is what the narrator's result, the metadata call, and the engine's memories read. `ruleset/consequences.py` → `generate_consequence_sentences` picks the sentence for the narrator from `engine/consequence_templates.yaml` by the kind and its values, never by reading the label back: a loss of health or spirit is light or heavy by `meters.heavy_loss_amount` in `ruleset/meters.yaml`, a bond's progress and a disposition shift are told about their NPC, a marked or cleared impact by its label, and a kind with no sentence of its own (progress, experience, a legacy reward, a follow-up move, an oracle row, a companion's meter) reaches the narrator only as its label.

**Datasworn mechanic naming.** Datasworn is the spec for mechanical behaviour, not for the shape of the Python. A Datasworn move becomes a formal move in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml` when it is a player choice the Brain can classify, with a structured outcome, or a move the engine makes as a follow-up, such as the suffer and threshold moves, which the Brain is never offered (`never` in `ruleset/move_availability.yaml`). A `no_roll` or `special_track` move that fires by itself when a mechanical condition becomes true becomes an engine trigger with a name that says what the code does, such as `advance_menace_on_miss`, and reaches the mechanic directly instead of going through the move-outcome pipeline.

**Shared outcome resolution.** Normal turns, corrections of a misread input, and momentum burns run the turn's own code in `game/turn.py`. Each turn keeps the snapshot taken right after its scene test, with the scene type, as `GameState.last_turn_snapshot`. A correction of a misread input restores it and plays the turn again with the corrected input (`game/turn.py` → `replay_turn`): same scene type, the facts already settled at that place, and the dice already rolled, rescored with the new stat, so a correction rolls only dice the turn did not roll: all of them when a dialog is corrected into a roll, and the action die when a progress roll is corrected into an action roll. A correction of the state edits it and narrates the turn again (`correction/orchestrator.py` → `_rewrite_prompt`) with what the turn told the narrator, its random events, threat events, filled clocks, and NPC agency, which the snapshot keeps (`TurnSnapshot.random_events`, `threat_events`, `clock_fill_results`, `npc_agency`), so nothing is drawn or ticked a second time; the analysis names NPCs by their id from the engine's list, and an operation the engine cannot apply (no NPC named, a time of day that does not exist) is skipped with a warning, and so is an empty name in an NPC's edit, whose other fields apply. A split that names no second figure rolls its name like any new NPC. A new chapter or a succession leaves nothing to correct, since its opening is not a turn. A momentum burn restores a second snapshot, taken right after the roll, resets momentum, and runs `resolve_and_narrate_action` with the better result (`game/momentum_burn.py` → `process_momentum_burn`). Every turn's narration, a correction's included, goes through `narrate_scene` and `apply_post_narration`; the openings and the epilogue call the narrator themselves.

**What a miss withholds.** A narrator asked to hold back on a miss still handed out clues, so the engine decides what it has (`prompt_action.py` → `build_action_prompt`). Every NPC present takes `information_gate.miss_stance` (withholding) instead of its matrix stance; the target NPC's gate falls to that stance's cap (`stance_caps.withholding`, 1), so it keeps only its description, with fact budget 0; bystanders lose their memory hint; the previous Director guidance waits for a hit, and no revelation lands (Blueprint below). A miss on a move that gathers information (`information_moves` in `ruleset/answers.yaml`, Gather Information) settles the place facts in `fact_resolution.information_miss_settles`, today `useful` no (`gamemaster/facts.py` → `settle_facts_by_miss`). A miss with a match rolls the story-complication oracle instead of Pay the Price where the setting has it (Starforged, Sundered Isles), as Datasworn describes that table. A hit grants in the same way: a target NPC whose stance from the matrix would refuse or deceive (`information_gate.refusing_stances`: a hostile NPC's hostile, stonewalling, confrontational, and misleading) takes `information_gate.hit_stance` instead, yielding, so a hostile NPC compelled or questioned on a hit gives what the roll won and keeps the hostility only in how (`gamemaster/stance_gate.py` → `resolve_npc_stance`); bystanders and turns without a roll keep the matrix's stance.

**Position and effect.** Blades in the Dark's position (controlled, risky, desperate) and effect (limited, standard, great) are computed by the engine for every roll, before its consequences (`gamemaster/resolvers.py` → `resolve_position`, `resolve_effect`, weights in `gamemaster/position_resolver.yaml` and `gamemaster/effect_resolver.yaml`, the chaos, streak, and clock thresholds in `chaos_resolver` of `gamemaster/chaos.yaml`). Position adds up a score from the strain on the character's meters, the target NPC's disposition and closeness, the chaos factor, a streak of failures or successes, threat clocks close to full, an advantage the previous turn gained, and a baseline per kind of action; two overrides cap it at risky (an advantage gained while a meter is critical) or raise it to risky (a recovery in crisis). Effect starts from position and adds closeness, the advantage, and the bonus the ruleset gives the action itself (Strike's, in `ruleset/answers.yaml`). Strain, closeness, the advantage, the kind of action, and how earlier actions went are the ruleset's answers (Game master and ruleset above). Position decides how many segments a miss ticks a threat clock (`gamemaster/clocks.yaml` → `miss_ticks_by_position`) and whether a weak hit ticks one: always when desperate, never when controlled, otherwise at the chance `pacing.weak_hit_clock_tick_chance`. Both reach the narrator as a `<position>` tag; effect changes nothing else.

## Progress tracks

`ProgressTrack` holds rank, ticks, and status (active, completed, failed, and bonded for a connection); ticks per mark by rank live in `ruleset/progress.yaml` under `ticks_per_mark`. The background vow becomes a track at creation, and the moves that create tracks are listed in `ruleset/track_moves.yaml` (`track_creating_moves`) and start them after the roll (`ruleset/tracks.py` → `start_track`): a vow, a fight, an expedition, or a journey whatever the result, a connection only on a hit (`tracks_started_on_a_hit`), as Make a Connection says. Undertake an Expedition and classic's Undertake a Journey, which both start a track and mark it, continue the track the Brain names in `target_track` and start one only when it names none. `ruleset/tracks.py` → `find_progress_track` and `complete_track` handle the lifecycle: completing a combat track clears the combat position, and completing a vow resolves its linked threat (`ThreatData.linked_vow_id`). A progress roll completes its track on a hit, strong or weak, as the moves have it (`ruleset/tracks.py` → `resolve_progress_roll`), except a connection, which Forge a Bond's hit bonds instead (`tracks_bonded_on_a_hit` in `ruleset/track_moves.yaml`); Forge a Bond's weak hit seals the bond at once and tells the narrator that they ask something more first (the `bond_request` effect). A bonded connection stays, as Starforged has it: it keeps its progress, so the NPC's bond holds, it aids with `bonded_add` instead of `add` (`ruleset/roll_bonuses.yaml`, the book's Bolster their influence), Make a Connection does not make it again (`ruleset/bond.py` → `connection_track` finds an active or bonded one), and `/tracks` names it (`bonded_tracks`). Develop Your Relationship follows its two branches: with a bond it rolls +the connection's rank for the bonds legacy track, and the Brain is offered it only while a bonded connection exists (`bonded_track` in `ruleset/move_availability.yaml`) and is refused it for an NPC without a bond, since `ruleset/roll_sources.py` → `roll_source_without_stat` rolls a rank only for a bonded connection; without a bond it marks progress per the connection's rank without a roll (`without_bond` in its entry in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`), which the engine does when Test Your Relationship's hit makes it as a follow-up (`ruleset/make_move.py` → `_chain_without_bond`). On a miss a fight is lost (End the Fight, Take Decisive Action), while Fulfill Your Vow, Forge a Bond, Finish an Expedition, and Reach Your Destination recommit: the track keeps one box in classic and loses as many boxes as the lower challenge die in Starforged, and its rank rises by one below epic (`recommit_on_miss` in the move's entry in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`, `ruleset/tracks.py` → `recommit_track`). Take Decisive Action in a bad spot counts a strong hit without a match as a weak hit and a weak hit as a miss (`downgrade_in`, `ruleset/rolls.py` → `downgrade_in_position`). A fight is its combat track: Enter the Fray sets who is in control on every result, completing the track ends the fight, and a successor starts outside it. A move's Datasworn track category maps to a track type through `ruleset/track_moves.yaml` → `track_types_by_category`, and where several tracks of that type are active, the Brain names the one the move is about by its track id (`target_track`); since move availability offers such a move only while its track exists, `find_progress_track` raises when it finds none. Datasworn gives a track category only to progress rolls, so an action move that marks progress (Strike, Clash, Gain Ground, Undertake an Expedition, Undertake a Journey, Delve the Depths, and the scene-challenge moves) names the category of its track in `progress_mark_categories` in the same file, taken from the progress roll of its own move category.

Scene challenges (Starforged, Sundered Isles) are configured but not playable yet: Begin the Scene has no roll and never reaches the Brain, so no scene-challenge track exists, the scene-challenge moves that need one are never offered, and the tension clock their misses should fill does not exist (roadmap step 9k).

Connection tracks carry an NPC's bond: a connection's track has the id of its NPC (`ruleset/bond.py` → `connection_track_id`), so Make a Connection needs the NPC as its target, and `get_npc_bond` reads that track's filled boxes; `NpcData` has no bond field. Two NPCs found to be one person, through a correction's merge or a duplicate absorbed after a rename (`gamemaster/npc/lifecycle.py` → `absorb_npc`), become one: the NPC that stays keeps the connection with the most progress and takes over the other's memories, clocks, and place on the characters list.

Track types, track statuses, and ranks are the ruleset's words, so code outside the ruleset names none of them and asks instead: `ruleset/tracks.py` for the active tracks, the chapter record's vows (`vow_record`), whether a thread follows a vow (`is_vow_thread`), the background vow a new character swears (`swear_background_vow`), and the end of a progress roll (`resolve_progress_roll`); `ruleset/bond.py` for a bonded connection and whether a move starts one; `ruleset/move_availability.py` for the moves a situation offers; and `ruleset/progress.py` → `ranks` for the ranks in order, which every table keyed by rank in the ruleset's yaml follows.

## Stats

A character's stats are the ruleset's: their names and descriptions come from the ruleset's Datasworn rules (`rules.stats`; Delve plays classic's, Sundered Isles Starforged's), read by `ruleset/stats.py` → `stat_descriptions`. A new character arranges the numbers of `creation_array` in `ruleset/stats.yaml` among them (`validate_stats`), and the creation form derives its total and range from that array. The Brain sees each stat with its description and the character's values by the abbreviations in the same file, and answers `ai_text.no_stat` in `engine/ai_text.yaml` for a move that rolls no stat.

## Meters

A character's meters are the ruleset's. Its condition meters come from the ruleset's Datasworn rules (`rules.condition_meters`, each with its range and starting value; Sundered Isles plays Starforged's, Delve classic's), and momentum from `ruleset/momentum.yaml`. `Resources` holds them by name (`Resources.meters`), with no meter as a field of its own, and `ruleset/meters.py` does their arithmetic: `lose` and `gain` keep a condition meter within its range, `adjust_momentum` keeps momentum between its floor and its max, which every marked impact lowers by one (`max_momentum`), so a gain stops at the lowered max, `reset_momentum` burns it to its starting value less one per impact, never below `reset_floor`, and `in_crisis` reads the meters `meters.crisis_at_zero` names in `ruleset/meters.yaml`. A move's effect, a suffer move, or a recovery acts on a meter by its name. A suffer or recovery move whose meter is no condition meter of the ruleset, such as a companion's health, changes only momentum, and a move effect naming such a meter raises; no move the Brain is offered names one yet (roadmap step 19). What the narrator and the player are told of a meter is keyed by its name: its descriptions and the flag the narrator gets while it stands at 0 in `engine/narrator_status_descriptions.yaml`, the player's in `status_descriptions.meters` in `engine/status_descriptions.yaml`, and the sentences of a gain or a loss in `engine/consequence_templates.yaml`, where a meter without a sentence reaches the narrator only as its label.

## Impacts, legacy, and assets

Impacts (wounded, shaken, and the rest) are the ruleset's own, read from its Datasworn rules with their label, the meters they keep from recovering, whether they are permanent, and their description (`ruleset/impacts.py` → `get_impacts`), and applied by `ruleset/impacts.py` → `apply_impact`, `clear_impact`, `blocks_recovery`. The narrator receives each marked impact with its description as `<impact>` in `<character_state>` (`prompt_shared.py` → `_impacts_block`). Every marked impact lowers max momentum by one (Meters above). They are marked by Endure Harm and Endure Stress, by Face Death and Face Desolation, and by supply running out (Harm, stress, and death below); on a hit Heal clears wounded and Starforged's Hearten and Resupply clear shaken and unprepared, classic Sojourn clears the oldest marked condition and takes +1 of its meter (the `clear_condition` effect), and a burden clears when its vow is completed. A marked condition blocks its meter's recovery in Endure's exchange and in the recovery moves' amounts, not in other gains (`docs/divergences.md`).

**Harm, stress, and death.** Every loss of health, spirit, or supply goes through `ruleset/suffer.py` → `suffer`, apart from the extra loss of Endure's own miss, which its handler takes: what the meter cannot take past 0 costs momentum, and supply reaching 0 marks unprepared (`ruleset/suffer.yaml` → `exhausted_impacts`). Lost health or spirit is then endured: in classic Ironsworn every time, as its Endure Harm and Endure Stress begin with the loss, and in Starforged and Sundered Isles when the meter stands at 0 (`endure_always` in the moves' params in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`). Enduring is a follow-up move: `suffer` lists it in `OutcomeResult.follow_up_moves`, and `ruleset/move_outcome.py` → `make_follow_up_moves` rolls it with the higher of its stat and its meter and resolves it through the suffer handler, after the turn's move, its chained move, its boasts, and any full menace track. A miss at 0 marks the first unmarked impact the move offers; when both are marked, the handler rolls the move's Datasworn table, tells its row, and acts on the rows `ruleset/suffer.yaml` → `table_rows` names: mortal harm and being overwhelmed make Face Death or Face Desolation, unconsciousness makes Face Death while a combat track is open, giving up forsakes the newest active vow (`ruleset/tracks.py` → `forsake_vow`), and dying starts a two-segment clock of type `dying`, which fills once per turn, action or dialog, after the turn's move, ends when health is above 0, and makes Face Death when full (`suffer.py` → `tick_dying_clock`). Face Death and Face Desolation roll +heart through the threshold handler: a strong hit returns, a miss ends the game, and a weak hit makes Swear an Iron Vow, whose outcome applies; in classic a miss on that vow ends the game too, and otherwise the character returns bound to an extreme vow (`start_bound_vow`) and marks its burden (cursed or tormented in classic, doomed or tormented in Starforged), which clears when the vow is completed. Health and spirit at 0 bring only the crisis status (`game/finalization.py` → `update_crisis`). In a dialog turn the dying clock, a full menace track, and their follow-up moves resolve before the narrator writes, and the narrator receives their labels as `<consequence>` tags and the status flags the action prompt has. The fixed choices this takes are in `docs/divergences.md`.

A ruleset's legacy tracks are the special tracks its Datasworn rules name and do not call optional, kept by name on the campaign (`CampaignState.legacy`, built by `ruleset/legacy.py` → `new_campaign`): Starforged's quests, bonds, and discoveries, and classic's bonds, which nothing marks yet (roadmap step 9j.2); Delve's optional failure track waits for step 13b, which marks it. Their numbers and experience are configured in `ruleset/legacy.yaml`: `ruleset/legacy.py` → `mark_legacy`, `apply_threat_overcome_bonus`, `advance_asset`. A legacy track earns experience as its boxes fill only where its Datasworn rules tag it so (`experience_tag`), as Starforged's three are; classic's bonds earn none, since classic earns experience by its own rule. The player spends experience with `/advance new <asset>` or `/advance upgrade <asset>`; `advance_asset` refuses an asset the setting lacks, a new asset already held, an upgrade of an asset not held or with every ability enabled, and an advance the experience does not cover, and the player hears which from `strings/advance.yaml`. Classic Ironsworn earns experience by its own rule: Fulfill Your Vow marks experience by the vow's rank, one rank lower on a weak hit (`experience_by_rank` in `ruleset/legacy.yaml`, applied by `ruleset/tracks.py` → `apply_progress_and_legacy`).

**Boasts.** Draw the Circle takes the boasts the player declares, which the Brain reads into its `boasts` field: up to two on a strong hit, one on a weak hit, none on a miss, each for +1 momentum and its cost from `ruleset/boasts.yaml` (`ruleset/boasts.py` → `apply_boasts`).

Assets: a path or asset has the abilities Datasworn enables by default until an upgrade enables the next in order, which `GameState.asset_abilities` records (`ruleset/assets.py`). `ruleset/bonuses.py` offers every enabled ability whose text grants an add, and the aid of every active or bonded connection (from `ruleset/roll_bonuses.yaml`), in a `<bonuses>` block of the Brain's prompt. The Brain names at most one by id in `bonus_id` when the action clearly meets its condition; the engine checks the id and takes the number from the rule text, counting momentum on a hit only when the text ties it to the same add. Only adds are modelled (`docs/divergences.md`).

## NPCs

**Memory and activation.** `gamemaster/npc/memory.py` scores memories for importance (`emotions/importance.yaml`, with keyword boosts from `emotions/keyword_boosts.yaml`), retrieves them, and consolidates them; a memory's emotional weight is derived from the kind of action, its outcome, and the disposition through `gamemaster/memory.yaml` (`gamemaster/engine_memories.py` → `derive_memory_emotion`). `gamemaster/npc/activation.py` scores NPCs against the scene with TF-IDF and decides which get full context in the prompt. That decides only whom the narrator's prompt holds: an NPC counts as having been in the scene, for its engine memory of the turn, the scene context, the Mythic characters list, and a reported death, when the activation picked it or found it mentioned and it is the action's target or the narration names it (`gamemaster/npc/matching.py` → `present_in_narration`); a reported death also counts for an NPC with a memory of this scene (`ai/metadata.py`). An NPC that only stands in the prompt's list of known NPCs does not count. Otherwise an NPC who had left would be remembered into every later scene, and the scene context naming it would activate it again. A text names an NPC when its name or one of its aliases stands there as whole words, or when a word of its proper name does: a word of the capitalised run that opens the name, unless a longer lowercase word follows that run, as in a descriptive name, and no title (`name_titles`) and no stopword, at least `fuzzy_match.named_in_min_word_length` letters long (`gamemaster/npc/matching.py` → `proper_name_words`, `named_in`). A proper name keeps its capital, so an NPC called Hope or Wolf is not named by "hope" or "a wolf", and a short name inside a longer word names nobody. A descriptive name or alias such as "the gate guard" names its NPC only as a whole, never by "gate" or "with" alone. Activation reads the player's own words without regard to case, since a player may type a name in lower case, and reads the Brain's and the scene's words the way it reads the narration (`gamemaster/npc/activation.py` → `activate_npcs_for_prompt`). The same test decides when the narration introduces an NPC (`parser.py` → `_mark_introduced_npcs`), which NPC an opening memory belongs to when `find_npc` finds none by id or name (`game/setup_common.py` → `seed_opening_memories`), and whether an NPC moves along to the player's location (`game/finalization.py` → `apply_engine_memories`).

**Stance.** The engine computes each NPC's stance from disposition, bond, and move category through `gamemaster/stance_matrix.yaml` (`gamemaster/stance_gate.py` → `resolve_npc_stance`). The target NPC and the activated NPCs reach the narrator with a behaviour, such as `stance="evasive" constraint="Answers in as few words as possible, then goes silent."`, instead of raw disposition values; the list of the other known NPCs names each with its disposition.

**Information gating.** A gate level from 0 (name and description, a stranger) to 4 (full secrets) decides what of the target NPC enters the narrator prompt (`gamemaster/stance_gate.py` → `compute_npc_gate`, points, caps, and budgets in `gamemaster/information_gate.yaml`, what each level shows in `prompt_shared.py` → `_npc_block`); an activated NPC shows its stance, arc, and a memory whatever its gate. The gate rises with scenes known, successful information gathering, and bond, and the stance caps it (`stance_caps`), so a hostile NPC opens no further than its agenda and a few memories, at gate 2, whatever the bond. The narrator cannot reveal what it does not have. The gate also sets how many new facts an NPC may volunteer in a reply.

**Names, lifecycle, agency.** New NPCs get oracle-rolled names from the setting's npc generator (Generators below), and `NpcData.name_rolled` records it. A full name the extraction reports for an NPC whose name the engine rolled, sharing no word with its name or aliases (the narrator's first label among them), becomes a new figure; for a figure the narration named, such as an opening NPC or a lore figure, the revealed name replaces the label, which stays as an alias (`gamemaster/npc/processing.py` → `_apply_name_update`). A new figure that matches a known NPC by its description is that NPC seen again: it keeps its name and gains the new label as an alias, and so does a figure named by whole words of a known NPC's name, such as a first name for a full name (`gamemaster/npc/description_match.py`, `gamemaster/npc/processing.py`). An NPC whose name the engine rolled is matched by its own name and by its earlier labels only: a figure that merely shares a word with such a label, such as "Second deckhand" beside "Lunging deckhand", is a new figure, and no figure's label replaces the rolled name, while a rename the extraction reports for its id does (`process_npc_renames`, roadmap step 11). The extraction names known NPCs for renames and details by id, from the ids its schema offers, and a death by a reference the engine looks up (`find_npc`), and a chapter or succession opening registers no NPC that the name matcher finds among the known ones (`game/setup_common.py` → `register_extracted_npcs`). `gamemaster/npc/processing.py` applies the extracted metadata, `gamemaster/npc/matching.py` matches names, and `gamemaster/npc/lifecycle.py` merges identities, retires, and reactivates NPCs. `gamemaster/clocks.py` → `check_npc_agency` tells the narrator every `pacing.npc_agency_interval` scenes that each active NPC with an agenda pursues it, and the narrator writes what the NPC does. The opening extraction gives the NPCs of an opening their agendas, instincts, and secrets, and the Director's reflections set them and the arcs later (`docs/ai.md`).

## Scenes, chaos, and random events

**Scene structure.** Every turn starts with a scene test, before the Brain call (`gamemaster/scene.py` → `check_scene`). The keyed branch runs first: if a `KeyedScene` on `narrative.keyed_scenes` has its trigger fire, the highest-priority match consumes itself and sets the scene type `keyed`, and its hint reaches the narrator as `<keyed_scene>`. Otherwise a d10 against the chaos factor decides: expected above it, altered at or below it on an odd roll, interrupt on an even one. Altered scenes roll on the Scene Adjustment Table; interrupt scenes generate a random event. The chaos factor moves per turn (`gamemaster/chaos.yaml`). Pacing is computed from the recent scene intensity (`gamemaster/pacing.py` → `get_pacing_hint`), not asked of the AI.

**Keyed scenes** are the engine's pre-scheduled beats. Trigger types are registered in `gamemaster/keyed_scenes.yaml` and evaluated through `gamemaster/keyed_scenes.py` → `_EVALUATORS`: `clock_fills`, `threat_menace_phase`, `bond_threshold`, `chaos_extreme`, `scene_count`. `gamemaster/keyed_scenes.py` → `add_keyed_scene`, through which every source adds a keyed scene under the next free id (`has_keyed_scene_from` tells a source whether it already added one), validates its trigger type, and for the three pattern triggers (`bond_threshold`, `threat_menace_phase`, `clock_fills`) its value, against the grammar in `pattern_grammars`: an NPC id, a threat's name, or a clock's name with `:N`, `any:N`, and for bonds `disposition_<value>:N`, each with a matcher strategy (`highest_bond`, `highest_menace`, `highest_filled`). When a pattern fires, `evaluate_keyed_scenes` binds the entity in `KeyedScene.bound_entity_id`, which nothing reads yet, since the fired scene leaves the list at once. Three sources spawn keyed scenes: the Adventure Crafter at chapter start (its `keyed_scene_mapping` maps 33 plot points to triggers, with at most one per plot point per chapter and a cap of `max_keyed_scenes_per_chapter`; a new chapter drops the ones the last chapter's turning points left), random events when they fire (from the event's target), and the opening clock and the clocks random events and plot points start (per clock type fractions from `gamemaster/clock_keyed_scenes.yaml`, through `gamemaster/clocks.py` → `spawn_keyed_scenes_for_clock`); complication clocks and the dying clock get none.

**Random events.** A four-step pipeline in `gamemaster/random_events.py`: event focus (a d100 over the focus categories, `roll_event_focus`), a target from the weighted Mythic lists, or for a PC Negative or Move Away event at `threat_target_probability` an open danger, a meaning-table roll on actions or descriptions (`roll_meaning_table`), and a structured `RandomEvent` (`generate_random_event`). Events fire on interrupt scenes and on fate doublets. They reach the narrator as `<random_event>` and `<interrupt_scene>` tags; an interrupt scene's own event appears only as `<interrupt_scene>`.

**Mythic lists.** The threads list starts with the background vow; the characters list starts with the vow's subject, if any, and the opening scene's NPCs. Both live on `NarrativeState`. Present NPCs gain weight. A thread gains one weight a scene when the scene invokes it, which the engine reads from what it can see (`game/scene_finalization.py` → `invoked_threads`): a random event aimed at the thread, a rolled move that acts on its vow (today Fulfill Your Vow; Reach a Milestone joins it once it is a formal move), and a threat linked to its vow that the scene advances or a random event aims at. New NPCs join the characters list as the NPC the engine named, and once `consolidation_threshold` entries of a list are active, every scene resets their weights (`gamemaster/random_events.py` → `add_thread_weight`, `add_character_weight`, `consolidate_lists`).

## Fate

Yes/no questions about the fiction, rolled by the engine with odds the engine sets (`gamemaster/fate.py`); the player never picks the odds, and a player's own question reaches fate only as a fact (see Ask the Oracle under Facts). Two methods: the fate chart (a 9×9 odds and chaos matrix, d100) and the fate check (2d10 plus modifiers), both with four answers (yes, no, exceptional yes, exceptional no); doubles whose digit is at most the chaos factor generate a random event, as Mythic's rule says. `fate.default_method` in `gamemaster/fate.yaml` picks the method, and the chaos factor enters only as the chart's column or the check's modifier, as Mythic intends. Both methods read Mythic's own tables in `data/mythic_gme_2e.json`: the chart's cells, and the check's odds and chaos modifiers and answer ranges. `gamemaster/fate.py` → `score_to_odds` turns a score into one of the nine odds through `fate.likelihood_rules.score_to_odds`, the single odds scale.

## Facts

A fact is something the fiction depends on that the game state does not hold, such as whether a door is locked or whether a guard is alert. The fact types live in `gamemaster/fact_resolution.yaml`: each names its subject (`place`, the current location, which the Brain writes as `here`, or `npc`, an NPC id from the Brain's list), a description that the Brain and the narrator both read, a base score, the game-state inputs that shift it with their weight, and whether a hit on an action that depended on the fact clears it. Today there are five: `locked`, `useful`, and `occupied` about the place, `present` and `alert` about an NPC, where `alert` weighs the NPC's disposition from `fate.likelihood_rules.disposition_scores` negatively, so a hostile guard is likely on guard and a friendly one unlikely. A `present` fact answered no about the NPC the action targets releases that target for the turn (`no_releases_target`, `gamemaster/facts.py` → `release_absent_target`), so the scene never holds the NPC both out of reach and as the one the action is aimed at.

The Brain names the undetermined facts an action or question turns on, at most `max_per_turn`, from the types and subjects its schema offers (`ai/schemas.py` → `get_brain_output_schema`). After the Brain's move to another place is applied, `gamemaster/generation.py` → `resolve_turn_facts` sends each through `generate_fact`, behind the categories registered in `gamemaster/generation.yaml`. `gamemaster/facts.py` → `resolve_fact` reuses a fact already settled at this place, or scores the fact type, converts the score to odds, asks `resolve_fate`, and stores the answer with the place it belongs to in `WorldState.facts`. The narrator receives the turn's facts as a `<facts>` block (`prompt_shared.py` → `_facts_block`, templates in `prompts/blocks.yaml`).

A fact belongs to the place where it was resolved. `gamemaster/world.py` → `clear_facts` empties the list when the player moves, when a correction changes the location, at a chapter start, and at a succession. After a roll, `gamemaster/facts.py` → `clear_facts_settled_by_hit` removes the facts of this turn that were answered yes and whose type says a hit settles them, so a door the player just forced is not reported locked again; the narrator still hears the fact this turn. A no stays after a hit, so searching the same place again does not roll the fact again, with one exception: a fact type that a miss on a search settles (`fact_resolution.information_miss_settles`, today `useful` no) is overruled by a hit on an action that turned on it (`overrule_facts_by_hit`). The narrator is then not told that nothing useful is here while the result says the search succeeds, and the place no longer holds that no. A momentum burn keeps the turn's facts and clears them by the better result; a correction of a misread input brings back the turn's facts of the same place (`remember_facts`) instead of rolling them again.

**Ask the Oracle.** The game master's move `ask_the_oracle` is the player's question about the fiction. A yes/no question that a fact type covers is answered by that fact, and the oracle table is not rolled; the narrator gets the `task_oracle_facts` task. A question for which the Brain names no fact, an open question or a yes/no question that no fact type covers, rolls the setting's action and theme oracle (`gamemaster/moves.py` → `roll_oracle_answer`) and reaches the narrator as `<oracle_answer>`.

## Generators

An entity category rolls the setting's own tables (`gamemaster/generation.py` → `generate_entity`). The categories are registered in `gamemaster/generation.yaml`, and each setting names under `generators` the table that every role of a category rolls (`docs/settings.md`). A table rolls a die as large as its highest row (`setting/loader.py` → `OracleTable.roll`), and a row of a shared table that has no roll range in its column, such as an atmosphere a furnace planet cannot have, is left out; Sundered Isles' highlands landmarks, ten rows that cover 1 to 50 of a d100, roll a d50. The roller follows Datasworn's own instructions on a row (`setting/oracle_rolls.py` → `roll_generator_tables`): an automatic roll replaces its link with what it rolled (an Action and Theme pair), a row that rolls its own table again rolls it as often as it says and treats a repeated row as its `duplicates` rule says, rolled again, kept, or made worse, as in classic's Pay the Price (`setting/oracle_rolls.py` → `roll_rows`, which Pay the Price, the chained oracle moves, and the names of complication clocks roll through as well), and a role's path may name an earlier role, which stands for the table that role's row points to or for its text, so Starforged's location theme picks the feature table of that theme and Sundered Isles' settlement location picks its focus table. A category that the active setting chain does not name raises.

Besides `fact`, three categories exist. `npc` rolls the name of a new NPC when the metadata extraction registers one (`gamemaster/npc/naming.py` → `roll_oracle_name`): given and family name, one name from classic's two hundred Ironlander names, or in Starforged the callsign at `naming.callsign_probability`. `location` and `settlement` are places. The Brain names the kind of every place it moves the character to, from `generation.place_categories`, and when the place is neither a generated place nor in the location history, `generate_place_on_arrival` rolls that category and keeps it as a `PlaceRecord` in `WorldState.places`: a theme and a feature in Starforged, a descriptor and a kind of place in classic Ironsworn, a region and a landmark in Sundered Isles, and for a settlement its setting's settlement tables (in classic only its trouble). While the character is at a generated place, the action and dialog prompts carry its rolls as a `<generated>` block (`prompt_shared.py` → `_generated_place_block`), and a return finds the same place. The name stays the Brain's. Places stay with the world across chapters and successions, and a correction of a misread input that goes to the same place keeps the place it generated. The rolled text already uses the setting's words, and the vocabulary instructions cover it like any other text, so no word is replaced in place. Nothing weights the tables by the world state yet (roadmap step 10).

## Threats and clocks

**Threats.** A threat has a menace track (`ruleset/threats.yaml`). `ruleset/threats.py` → `advance_menace_on_miss` advances the menace of a vow's linked threat on a miss, `tick_autonomous_threats` ticks threats on their own, and `resolve_full_menace` handles a full track through `ruleset/tracks.py` → `forsake_vow`; all three are engine rules that depart from Delve's Advance a Threat and from Forsake Your Vow (`docs/divergences.md`). A threat gets its vow as Delve has it, once it is introduced and the player swears a vow to see its goal undone: the Brain sees the active threats in `<dangers>`, each with its menace and the vow sworn against it, and names in `target_threat` the one a vow it swears is against, from those without one, and `ruleset/tracks.py` → `start_track` links the new vow to it (`ruleset/threats.py` → `vow_against`). A full track is handled while a turn resolves, action or dialog, before the narrator writes, so the narrator tells the forsaken vow; a track that its own tick fills at scene end waits for the next turn. Every advance reaches the narrator as `<threat_advance>`: one on a miss in the same turn, one a random event aims at before that turn's roll, and an autonomous tick, which comes after narration, in the next turn, marked as off screen (`WorldState.pending_threat_events`, `take_pending_threat_events`). Where the setting has a table for the threat's category (Delve's nine, under `threats.category_oracle_collection`), the advance carries a roll on it as what the threat does.

Threats also spawn from two sources, which ask the ruleset for a new danger (Game master and ruleset above): random events in play, and the turning points a chapter's blueprint rolls at its start. A random event whose focus is listed in `gamemaster/random_events.yaml` → `danger_foci` (currently `pc_negative` and `npc_negative`) names the danger after the event's Mythic action and subject (`gamemaster/random_events.py` → `spawn_danger_from_random_event`). An Adventure Crafter plot point listed in `crafters/adventure_crafter.yaml` → `danger_plot_points` (A New Enemy, Hidden Threat, Enemies, Hunted, A Problem Returns) leaves the name to the ruleset, which rolls a Datasworn cascade on the setting's `oracle_paths.threats` (`ruleset/threats.py` → `add_threat`). The cascade (`setting/cascade.py` → `roll_oracle_cascade`) rolls each table with its rows' own roll instructions (a "Roll twice" row gives both results) and follows the first markdown-link id onward: Delve's `threat/category` leads into nine sub-tables, and the one it reaches is the threat's category, while Starforged, classic Ironsworn, and Sundered Isles take the category that `threats.by_origin` in `ruleset/threats.yaml` gives the plot point, beside the rank of every origin. The ruleset dedupes on `ThreatData.creation_source` and lets both sources spawn a threat only while fewer than `threats.max_open_emergent` threats they spawned are still active. A threat no vow is sworn against does not advance on a miss, but it ticks on its own and can fire `threat_menace_phase` keyed scenes.

**Clocks.** A new game starts with one threat clock named after the background vow, sized and filled from `gamemaster/opening.yaml` (`gamemaster/clocks.py` → `start_opening_clock`), and every opening (new game, chapter, succession) starts at the time of day that file names; the AI's opening extraction sets neither. Later clocks spawn the same way as threats. A random event whose focus is in `gamemaster/random_events.yaml` → `clock_creation_mapping` (currently `pc_negative` and `move_away_from_thread` to a threat clock, `move_toward_thread` to a progress clock) names the clock after its Mythic pair; an Adventure Crafter plot point in `crafters/adventure_crafter.yaml` → `clock_creation_mapping` (Time Limit, A Crucial Life Support System Begins To Fail, A Needed Resource Runs Out, Impending Doom) names it after the plot point. Size and owner come from `gamemaster/clocks.yaml` (`default_segments`, `default_owner_kind`), these spawners and the complication clocks below spawn a clock only while fewer than `max_open_emergent` clocks they made are still unfired, and every new clock from these two spawners gets its keyed scenes at once (`gamemaster/clocks.py` → `spawn_emergent_clock`, which also skips a source whose clock is still listed; a fired clock leaves the list after `pacing.fired_clock_keep_scenes` scenes). Every clock's code lives in `gamemaster/clocks.py`: making, ticking, and filling it; the exceptions are the dying clock, a timer of the ruleset's Endure Harm that `ruleset/suffer.py` keeps (Harm, stress, and death), and what a filled progress clock does to a track, which the ruleset answers (`ruleset/tracks.py` → `complete_track_of_clock`). Only threat clocks tick: a miss ticks the first open one in the list, the scene end each world-owned one by chance, and NPC agency only a clock an NPC owns, which no clock does until step 12; nothing ticks a progress clock yet, and no scheme clock is made (step 14b). The spawn-source prefixes are constants in `gamemaster/spawn_sources.py`.

**Complication clocks.** The user's idea for a complication that begins now and strikes only if the player leaves it, after Blades in the Dark, which turns a complication into a clock (`gamemaster/clocks.py` → `spawn_complication_clock`). A weak hit with a `complication` effect starts a world-owned threat clock of `clocks.complication.segments` (4), named by a roll on `name_oracle_path` (the story-complication table where the setting has it) or else by a Mythic action pair, unless `max_open` (1) complication clocks are open or the cap on open spawned clocks is reached; the narrator gets the danger as a consequence to show as a first sign. It fills one segment at every scene end, the scene that started it included (`tick_chance` 1.0 in `gamemaster/clocks.py` → `tick_autonomous_clocks`), and a miss's tick reaches it once every threat clock before it in the list is full, so it fills within four scenes and strikes through the threat fill tag, which a fill at scene end brings to the next turn. It has no keyed scenes, so the tag that the danger has arrived is what the narrator tells. The Brain sees open complication clocks as `<dangers>` and names one in `target_clock` when the action works to stop, undo, or escape it; a hit on that action removes the clock and tells the narrator it was headed off (`game/action_resolution.py` → `_maybe_avert_complication`).

**A filled clock** makes `gamemaster/clocks.py` → `resolve_clock_fill` emit a `<clock_filled>` tag from the per-type templates in `gamemaster/clocks.yaml` → `fill_consequences` (`threat`, `scheme`, `progress`), and the ruleset completes the active progress track of a filled progress clock's name, unless a keyed scene from that clock is still pending, which then takes precedence over both. Since every clock that random events and plot points start gets a keyed scene at its full mark, in play only complication clocks emit the tag. Filled threat and scheme clocks do not advance menace. Fills from ticks on a miss reach the same turn's narrator prompt; fills from the autonomous ticks after narration queue on `WorldState.pending_clock_fills` and open the next turn's prompt.

## Story structure

**Blueprint.** At the start of a chapter `crafters/blueprint.py` → `assemble_blueprint_seed_from_ac` rolls one of the Adventure Crafter's turning points for each act of `blueprint.act_phases` (`crafters/adventure_crafter.py` → `roll_turning_point`) into a frozen `BlueprintSeed`, and `ai/blueprint_voicing.py` → `call_blueprint_voicing` turns it into a setting-specific `StoryBlueprint` in one structured call, which asks for a fixed number of acts, of revelations (one per act), and of possible endings and asks once more when the answer's counts are wrong (`docs/ai.md`); when the voicing fails, the chapter plays without a blueprint, and so without acts, revelations, or an end. Each act's turning point reaches the voicing with its beats, its plotline, and whether it opens, advances, or concludes that plotline, as the Adventure Crafter rolled it (`TurningPoint.plotline`, `opens_plotline`, `concludes_plotline`). After each turning point, `crafters/turning_point_spawns.py` adds its keyed scenes, dangers, and clocks. The player picks a tone at character creation, from `story.tones` and preset to the setting's `default_tone`, and the narrator receives it as its tone authority. The Adventure Crafter is not consulted in the middle of a chapter; pacing there runs on random events, the pacing hint, clocks, threats, the scene structure, and the Director's reflections.

**Revelations.** Each act has one revelation, seeded from a plot point of its own turning point: the first that has a keyed scene once the chapter's turning points have spawned theirs, else the first that is no special result (Conclusion, None, Meta), and with neither, the voicing takes it from the central conflict. The engine decides when it lands (`story_state.py` → `land_revelation`): in its act, when its keyed scene fires, which makes it ready at once if that happened in an earlier act, and at the latest on the last scene of its act; and only on a turn whose outcome `story_state.revelation_outcomes` in `gamemaster/story.yaml` names, today every outcome but failure, so a failure holds it to the next turn that does not fail, even past its act. One lands per turn, the oldest first. The narrator receives it as a `<revelation>` tag beside the consequences, which it must tell, the engine marks it revealed in the same turn, and the Director runs after it (`docs/ai.md`). A momentum burn decides again with the better result, a correction of a misread input plays the turn again, and a state correction tells the same revelation again (`TurnSnapshot.revelation`).

Act transitions are engine-computed at the end of every turn, whether or not the Director runs: an act ends at the last scene of its range (`story_state.py` → `check_act_transition`, called in the scene-end bookkeeping), so the Director after that turn and the next turn's prompts already see the next act (`get_current_act`); `check_story_completion` then decides whether the story is complete.

**Adventure Crafter tables.** The primitives in `crafters/adventure_crafter.py`, with engine config in `crafters/adventure_crafter.yaml` and data in `data/adventure_crafter.json`: five themes from a d10 table, plot points looked up by theme and roll, the special ranges Conclusion (1 to 8), None (9 to 24), and Meta (96 to 100), and in `crafters/meta_plot_points.py` the handlers of the seven meta plot points. The yaml `theme_die_table` is checked against the JSON's `random_themes` on first load. `roll_turning_point` combines two to five plot points: a roll on `plotlines_list_template` opens a new plotline on New Plotline or when no plotline advances, and otherwise takes the advancing plotline with the fewest turning points (`docs/divergences.md`), then per plot point a theme by `plot_point_theme_priority` (alternating for the fourth and fifth) and 2d10 for the lookup. A Conclusion flips the plotline from advancement to conclusion, and counts as None on a plotline the turning point just opened. `NarrativeState` holds the `characters_list`, shared with Mythic's random events and extended with `ac_status`, and the Adventure Crafter's own `plotlines_list`; the meta handlers change both in place, but no turning point calls them yet, so a Meta result has no effect (`docs/divergences.md`); `dispatch_meta` and the Characters List template wait under an orphan-symbol carve-out for roadmap step 9j.7. The character-crafting tables are pure helpers (`roll_character_traits`, whose `CharacterTraits` holds one or two identities and descriptors), under the same carve-out until roadmap step 11.

## Chapters and succession

**Chapter transitions.** A chapter is what Mythic and the Adventure Crafter call an adventure within a campaign: one blueprint. It ends by scene count, not when its plotlines are resolved: the story is complete once the scene count reaches the end of the last act's range, an earlier act that has not ended yet ending then too; `story_state.completion_overrun_scenes` scenes past that end, a fallback, matters only for a blueprint of one act (`story_state.py` → `check_story_completion`), and then the player is offered the epilogue and a new chapter (roadmap step 32b reads this against the books). `game/chapters.py` → `start_new_chapter` closes the old chapter into a `ChapterSummary`, with a record the engine writes from its own data (vows fulfilled, forsaken, and still sworn, plotlines concluded, open threads, people with disposition and bond, the dead, threats with their menace and those overcome, where the chapter ended, opening with the blueprint's central conflict; the title is the chapter's number). What belongs to one chapter starts fresh in `_reset_chapter_mechanics`: the scene count, the chaos factor at Mythic's starting value of 5, the session log and narration history, the blueprint and the keyed scenes its turning points spawned, the time of day, the location history, the facts, and the Director's guidance. Everything else carries over as it stands: progress tracks, threats, impacts, assets, threads, the characters and plotlines lists, health, spirit, supply, momentum, and clocks, since Ironsworn and Starforged recover only through moves. Experience and legacy tracks span the whole campaign, and the generated places stay with the world; NPCs and their connection tracks carry over through `game.npcs` and `_prepare_npcs_for_new_chapter`. A game that is over continues through succession, never a new chapter.

**Character succession.** When the protagonist dies (on a miss at Face Death or Face Desolation, or in classic on a miss on the vow sworn there; Harm, stress, and death above) or retires, which the game records as its `end_reason` (death, despair, or retire; the game is over while it is set), the campaign continues with a new protagonist in the same world, in two steps gated by `CampaignState.pending_succession`. `game/succession.py` → `prepare_succession` archives the predecessor into `campaign.predecessors` and locks the inheritance rolls onto that record, which keeps inheritance the same across a reload. `start_succession_with_character` reads those rolls, closes the chapter, carries NPCs and their connection tracks over per `ruleset/succession.yaml`, and keeps every threat and the threads that were not the character's vows or creation, and of the characters list the NPCs who stay. The rest goes: resources, impacts, assets, and the other progress tracks leave with the character, every clock is cleared with its keyed scenes, unlike at a chapter start, the keyed scenes of the old chapter's turning points go as at a chapter start, a fight ends with its combat position, and the scene count, chaos factor, time of day, and facts restart. It then seeds the successor's legacy, replaces the character, writes the opening, and clears the flag. The mechanics live in `ruleset/succession.py` → `build_predecessor_record`, `run_inheritance_rolls`, `seed_successor_legacy`, `apply_npc_carryover`. Unknown roll outcomes and NPC statuses raise.
