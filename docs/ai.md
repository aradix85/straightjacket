# The AI layer

Which AI roles exist, which model runs them, and how calls are routed, cached, streamed, and allowed to fail. Why the AI narrates and does not decide is in ARCHITECTURE.md.

## Roles

Every AI call carries its own role name (`AICallSpec.log_role`), and every role maps to one cluster in `config.yaml`.

- `brain` turns the player's input into a move, a stat, a roll bonus, a target NPC, a track by id, a complication clock to head off, the threat a vow it swears is against, a new track's name and rank, the undetermined facts the action or question turns on, and the boasts the player declares in a duel (`ai/brain.py` → `call_brain`). Its free text is the action's `approach` and `player_intent`, which activation, the dialog prompt, and the engine's memories read, a new track's name, the name in `location_change`, the place the character moves to, which comes with the place's kind from the engine's place categories and sets the location and the time of day, clears the old place's facts, and lets the engine generate a place the world does not know yet (`docs/mechanics.md`, Generators), and `world_addition`, a physical detail the player's words add to the scene, which reaches the narrator. The game state is injected into its prompt, and its output schema offers only the available moves, the stats and ways to roll, the offered bonuses, the listed NPCs, the active tracks by id, the ranks, the open complication clocks by name, the active threats no vow is sworn against, the place kinds, the boasts, and the fact types with the subjects each allows; `call_brain` refuses anything else, an empty track name for a move that starts a track, a move that makes a connection without a target NPC, a target track of another kind than the progress move works on, and a move whose outcomes act on an NPC (a bond's progress, sealing or clearing a bond, a connection's rank, a disposition) without a target NPC (`ruleset/move_outcome.py` → `acts_on_an_npc`), since such an effect has nothing to act on and raises, and one that marks, raises, or clears a connection for a target NPC without one (`needs_a_connection`). Each move reaches it with its ways to roll in the book's words, a way that picks among its stats answered by the method's name (`docs/mechanics.md`, Moves and outcomes). A Datasworn action move answered with a stat it does not offer, or with `none`, and `world_shaping` answered with a way to roll, are asked again once with the move's stats named (`retry.brain_stat_retries`), and a second wrong answer fails the turn, which rolls back. A move that rolls with a meter or a connection's rank takes its value from the game state whatever stat the Brain names, and `call_brain` refuses it when its target has nothing to roll with; `world_shaping` without a stat plays as dialog (`game/turn.py` → `_sanitize_brain_output`). The Brain names a fact; the engine decides it (`docs/mechanics.md`, Facts).
- `narrator` writes the prose, with conversation memory (`ai/narrator.py`).
- `narrator_metadata` reads the finished narration and extracts NPC data (new NPCs with a disposition from the engine's list, renames and details of known NPCs by their id, deaths, and figures who are only spoken of), which `ai/metadata.py` applies to the game state.
- `opening_setup` extracts the NPCs with their dispositions, agendas, instincts, and secrets, their first memories, the location, and the scene context from the opening of a new game, a chapter, or a succession; the opening clock and the time of day are the engine's (`docs/mechanics.md`).
- `recap` and `blueprint_voicing` write the player-facing recap and a setting-specific story blueprint from an Adventure Crafter seed (`ai/recap.py`, `ai/blueprint_voicing.py`). The chapter summary kept in campaign history is built by the engine (`docs/mechanics.md`, Chapters and succession).
- `director` writes NPC reflections and guidance for the narrator after a turn that calls for it (`director.py` → `should_call_director`): a miss, a scene that was not the expected one, new NPCs, a revelation that landed (`docs/mechanics.md`, Revelations), an NPC due a reflection, the first turn of the climax, every `pacing.director_interval` scenes, and once after the epilogue; after a state correction it runs when the analysis calls it useful and these triggers agree. What it decides came with EdgeTales and departs from the design document; roadmap step 11.0 settles what it keeps (`docs/divergences.md`, An AI Director steers the story and the NPCs).
- `correction` analyses a `##` correction (`correction/analysis.py` → `call_correction_brain`): a misread input, which the engine replays with the corrected input and the dice already rolled, or state operations, whose schema offers the operations and fields in `engine/correction.yaml` and the NPCs by id.

The narrator writes pure prose; everything structured comes from separate calls with strict JSON schemas (`ai/schemas.py`). Why narration and extraction are separate calls is recorded in `docs/divergences.md`. Prompts live in `prompts/*.yaml`, one key per role or task: the narrator's system prompt, the two extraction prompts with their requests, and the recap in `prompts/narrator.yaml`; the narrator's task texts in `prompts/tasks.yaml` and its blocks in `prompts/blocks.yaml`; the Brain and the correction analysis in `prompts/brain.yaml`; the Director in `prompts/director.yaml`; blueprint voicing in `prompts/blueprint_voicing.yaml`. They are tuned for the models in use: switching models means re-tuning in place, not adding parallel variants.

## Model assignment

`config.yaml` names, under `ai.clusters`, each cluster's provider, model, and call parameters, in full even where clusters repeat each other, and maps every role to exactly one cluster under `ai.role_cluster`. The clusters are narrator, creative (blueprint voicing, recap), director, classification (brain, correction), and extraction (narrator metadata, opening setup). An AI call reaches them only through `model_for_role(role)`, `provider_for_role(role)`, and `sampling_params(role)`, and the adapters and the startup check read the clusters themselves (`ai/api_client.py`); no module hardcodes a model. A `null` temperature or top_p is not sent, and an `api_base` of `""` means the SDK's default endpoint. A key is needed only for a provider in use.

Every cluster runs Claude Sonnet 5.5 through Anthropic at effort `medium` (`output_config` in the cluster's `extra_body`), with its adaptive thinking, which is on by default: one model for every role, the user's choice, since quality comes from the engine's structure and the prompts. The clusters send no `temperature` or `top_p`, since Sonnet 5.5 refuses any value other than the default, so the roles differ in prompt and effort.

`ai/api_client.py` → `_schema_in_prompt` appends the compact JSON schema (template `json_schema_instruction` in `prompts/blocks.yaml`) to the end of the last message of every call with a schema, which is always the user's, because a model that does not see the schema may write whitespace until the token limit, which a JSON grammar allows; it goes last, so every cached prefix stays intact.

**Caching.** Hosts cache the longest identical prefix of a prompt, so prompts put their fixed text first and what changes per turn last: the narrator system prompt its fixed rules, then the blocks fixed for a game, then the character state; the Brain its rules and the world ahead of the moves on offer; the Director its fixed task, the world, and the character ahead of the scene. Anthropic caches only up to a marked block, and its automatic mark falls on the last block of the request, which changes every turn, so `ai/provider_anthropic.py` → `_cached_system` sends the system prompt as two blocks and marks the first, the part fixed within a game (`AICallSpec.cached_system_prefix`, cut by `prompt_loader.py` → `get_prompt_prefix` at the first placeholder that changes per turn: `status_context_block` for the narrator, `moves_block` for the Brain); a call without a prefix marks its whole system prompt. The cluster's `cache_control` turns this on and sets the cache's lifetime. The adapter does not set Anthropic's automatic mark as well (a top-level `cache_control`): that would write the part of every request that changes each turn at the cache-write price, twice the input price for a one-hour cache, and no later request reads it back. Both adapters report the cached share as `cache_read_tokens`, which the `[TOKENS]` log line shows, and the OpenAI-compatible adapter the reasoning share as `reasoning_tokens` where the host reports it, which the Director's error for an answer that is not JSON names.

## Routing and the startup check

`ai/api_client.py` → `get_provider` returns a routing provider that sends each call to the provider of its role's cluster, keyed on `AICallSpec.log_role`; a project-rule scan requires every AI call to set it. `python run.py` and Elvira call `check_configured_models` first: every cluster's model must appear in its provider's model list, or startup stops and names the missing model. It reads each OpenAI-compatible provider's `/models` with a plain request, because Together returns a bare list that the OpenAI SDK's model listing cannot parse.

## Provider adapters

The `AIProvider` protocol has two implementations, `ai/provider_anthropic.py` and `ai/provider_openai.py` (any OpenAI-compatible endpoint); nothing else imports a provider SDK. Both create their SDK client with `max_retries=0` and the provider's `timeout_seconds`, so `ai/provider_base.py` → `create_with_retry` is the only retry layer: it retries a connection error, which the SDKs' timeout error is, and an HTTP status in `retry.retryable_http_codes`, honours a `Retry-After` header, capped by `retry.max_retry_after_seconds` in `engine/retry.yaml`, and otherwise backs off exponentially. Its catch covers the provider's call as a whole, so a fault in the request that the router or an adapter builds inside that call (the role's provider, the schema instruction, the prompt-cache split) fails the call like an unreachable AI, which fails the turn for the Brain and the narrator and degrades every other call; such a fault repeats on every call of its role, and the log names its type and message, which Elvira reports. Keeping the request apart would add a step to the provider protocol that every provider and test provider implements, without showing a fault any better. A refusal (Anthropic `refusal`, OpenAI `content_filter`) becomes the stop reason `refusal`, which is asked again at once, without a wait, up to the call's retries, and a refused stream is asked again without streaming. Only text reaches `AIResponse.content`, never reasoning; the OpenAI-compatible adapter keeps a host's reasoning text apart for diagnostics on a call that does not stream. The OpenAI-compatible adapter sends `max_completion_tokens`, which OpenAI's own models require. The Anthropic adapter sends `temperature` and `top_p` only when set, routes `thinking` and `output_config` from the cluster's `extra_body` to their typed parameters, and puts `cache_control` on the system prompt's first block (Caching above). It passes a call's JSON schema through the SDK's `transform_schema`, because Anthropic's structured outputs refuse array limits such as `maxItems` (and numeric and string limits); the SDK moves them into the field's description, and the engine checks the counts itself after parsing (`ai/brain.py` → `_check_choices` for facts and boasts, blueprint voicing for its acts, revelations, and endings, and `ai/narrator.py` → `call_narrator_metadata` for renames and details while no NPC is known, which fail the extraction).

## When an AI call fails

The two calls a turn cannot do without, the Brain and the narrator, do not degrade. After the retries they raise `ai/provider_base.py` → `AIUnavailableError`, as do a persistent refusal, an answer of the Brain's that `call_brain` refuses, and an empty narration, or one of which `parser.py` → `parse_narrator_response` leaves nothing after cleanup. A narration cut at the token limit loses its unfinished last sentence (`parser.py` → `salvage_truncated_narration`); one the provider reports as complete is kept whole. The web handler for a turn, a correction, or a momentum burn then restores the snapshot it took before the call and tells the player that nothing in the story changed, as the design document asks (`web/handlers.py` → `_restore_after_failed_turn`, `_report_failure`); a new chapter and a succession run on a copy of the game, which replaces it only when the opening succeeds, so a failed opening leaves the game as it was. A handler catches only the call that reaches the AI; `web/server.py` → `_dispatch_one_message` logs any other fault in a handler at error level and tells the player an unexpected error occurred.

Every other call degrades without a wrong state change: the metadata extraction and the Director are skipped, the opening extraction registers no NPCs and leaves the location and scene context as they were, a failed correction analysis is treated as a state correction without operations, so no operation applies and the turn is narrated again with the player's correction text as guidance, followed by the extraction and memories every narration gets, blueprint voicing returns None (after asking once more when its counts are wrong, `adventure_crafter.blueprint.voicing_count_retries`), and a recap uses its fallback. Each such site logs at warning or error level, which Elvira reports as a problem (`docs/elvira.md`). The broad exception handling this needs is the one exception to "errors propagate" in CONTRIBUTING.md.

## Narration streaming

With `server.stream_narration: true` in `config.yaml`, a player turn streams the narrator's output. `web/handlers.py` hands `process_turn` a `SentenceStream`, which travels through the turn into `call_narrator`; `ai/provider_base.py` → `stream_with_retry` calls the provider's `stream_message`, and only text deltas reach the stream. The stream buffers text until a sentence is complete (abbreviations and hold markers live in `engine/parser.yaml`), cleans it with `parser.py` → `clean_sentence`, and the handler sends it as a `narration_sentence` message that the client appends to the log. The `narration` message still carries the authoritative, fully parsed text and a `stream_complete` flag: when the streamed text matches, the client leaves it; otherwise it replaces it and announces only the part the player has not heard. A hold marker (`stream_hold_markers`: a tag, a code fence, JSON, a rule, a heading) stops the stream and leaves the rest to the final message; a failed stream, the sentence stream's own handling of a delta included, falls back to a normal call. Openings, corrections, and momentum burns do not stream.

## Tool calling and prompt injection

Prompt injection here means placing data in the prompt; the attack of the same name is in SECURITY.md. Prompt injection fits when the data is always or nearly always relevant for the call and its size is bounded; a tool round there only adds overhead. Tool calling fits when the data is selective and the AI is best placed to choose, when the full set is too large to inject (the Datasworn oracle tables, whole memory histories), or when the need depends on the AI's judgment. Tool calling is wrong where the AI must not choose: the Brain cannot pick which moves are available or which NPCs are present, so it gets everything by prompt injection. The narrator gets the turn's resolved facts the same way, as a `<facts>` block, because they are always relevant for the call that triggered them and bounded by `max_per_turn`. New AI-consumable data is placed on the same axis.

Every role is prompt-only except the Director. Its prompt carries the scene, the story arc with its conflict, antagonist, and theme, its own last `arc_notes` (the observation on the arc each answer ends with, which the next call builds on), and a reflection block for each NPC the engine selected; its answer's schema gives each of those NPCs exactly one reflection slot keyed by NPC id, and a reflection for any other NPC is rejected. It has one tool, `tools/builtins.py` → `query_game_state`, which returns the active threads, the unfired clocks, and every active or background NPC in one answer. Tools are registered with `@register` in `tools/registry.py`, which takes each tool's description from `engine/tool_descriptions.yaml`; they take no parameters, read the game state, and never mutate. The Director's first call offers the tool; `director.py` → `call_director` runs each call it asks for through `tools/handler.py` → `execute_tool_call` and hands the raw results, as `<tool_results>`, to the second call, which writes the structured answer. A tool the model invents answers with an error, and arguments it passes anyway are ignored and named in the result; both log a warning. A fault inside a tool is the engine's and propagates. There is no round in between, since the Director needs the data, not the model's comment on it, and given one Claude Sonnet 5.5 called tools that do not exist ("respond", "reflect"). Anthropic's structured outputs allow at most 16 nullable or union-typed fields in one schema, so the Director's reflection fields are plain strings, empty when there is nothing to set; `tests/test_schema_strictness.py` keeps every schema under that limit. The reflection blocks and the tool read an NPC's recent memory through one function, `gamemaster/npc/memory.py` → `recent_observations` (its last observations, `npc.reflection_observation_window` in `gamemaster/npc.yaml`), so it looks the same on both paths.

## Adding a new AI provider

1. Create `ai/provider_yourname.py` implementing the `ModelListingProvider` protocol (`create_message`, `stream_message`, and `list_models`, see `ai/provider_base.py`).
2. Add a branch for its type in `ai/api_client.py` → `build_adapter`, and the type to the valid types its error names.
3. Add an entry under `ai.providers` in `config.yaml` with that `type`, and point clusters at it with `provider:`.

A service with an OpenAI-compatible endpoint needs only step 3, with `type: openai_compatible`.
