# Deliberate divergences

Where Straightjacket departs on purpose from the rulebooks it draws on, or from the design document it implements. Everything not listed here follows its source.

## From the source rulebooks

A rule taken from Ironsworn/Starforged, Mythic GME 2e, the Adventure Crafter, or Blades in the Dark works as its source describes, or it is listed below. `tests/test_rules_conformance.py` pins the checked rules, including an automated comparison of every unconditional momentum gain in the Datasworn move texts with `engine/move_outcomes.yaml`.

- **Chaos factor per turn.** Mythic adjusts the chaos factor once per scene, by whether the player characters were in control. Straightjacket treats each turn as a scene and reads control from the roll: a miss raises the chaos factor by one, a strong hit lowers it by one, a weak hit leaves it, and dialog moves it by the NPC's disposition (`engine/chaos.yaml`). Start 5 and range 1 to 9 follow Mythic.
- **Classic Ironsworn uses Starforged's impact model.** `engine/move_outcomes.yaml` follows Starforged, and its `move_outcome_overrides` hold the classic differences (Secure an Advantage, Endure Harm, Endure Stress), so the numbers follow each rulebook. Classic Endure Harm and Endure Stress mark Ironsworn's own lasting harm (`maimed`, `corrupted`; `engine/impacts.yaml` also knows `encumbered`). What classic still borrows from Starforged is that wounded or shaken blocks recovery, where Ironsworn only asks for health or spirit above 0.
- **Classic Ironsworn earns experience through Starforged's legacy tracks.** Ironsworn marks experience directly when a vow is fulfilled; Straightjacket marks the quests, bonds, and discoveries legacy tracks in every setting and earns experience per filled box.
- **Make a Connection shifts the NPC's disposition** on a strong hit, an engine addition with no rulebook number behind it; the move itself gives no momentum and no progress.
- **Fixed choices where the player would choose a cost.** Where an outcome lets the player pick a cost from a list (Take Decisive Action and End the Fight on a weak hit, classic Reach Your Destination on a miss), the engine pays the price instead.
- **Pay the Price rolls the official table and costs 1.** `engine/pay_the_price.yaml` names the setting's Datasworn table; a row that says the character is harmed, stressed, or wastes resources costs 1 health, spirit, or supply, the smallest suffer amount, where the rules leave the size to the player.
- **A chained move is rolled at once, and only if it has a roll.** Where an outcome says to make another move (Test Your Relationship's hits say Develop Your Relationship), the effect `chain_move` makes `game/finalization.py` roll that move in the same turn, with the best stat its roll options allow or, for a connection move, the connection's rank, spending a banked next-move bonus; its consequences join the first move's for the narrator. A chained move without a roll is an oracle move configured under `oracle_moves` in `engine/move_outcomes.yaml`: the engine rolls its table and marks its legacy ticks at once, where the rules mark them when the discovery or aspect is first engaged. Confront Chaos lets the player choose one to three aspects; the engine takes one.
- **Roll bonuses are chosen for the player.** The Brain decides whether an action meets a bonus's condition and names at most one bonus per roll; an asset upgrade enables its abilities in order, where the player would choose; only adds are modelled (the mechanism is in `docs/mechanics.md`).
- **Boasts are not modelled.** Draw the Circle grants its weak-hit momentum without the boast the player would choose.
- **A fate-chart roll of 100 counts as doubles** and triggers a random event; Mythic 2e does not settle this edge case.
- **Yes/no questions go through Mythic's fate chart, with odds the engine derives.** Ironsworn's Ask the Oracle and Mythic's fate question both let the player choose the odds. Straightjacket answers the facts an action or question depends on through Mythic's fate chart, so the chaos factor weighs in as Mythic intends and there is one odds scale, and derives the odds from the fact type and the game state (`engine/fact_resolution.yaml`), because the engine, not the player, decides what the fiction holds. Only the fact types listed there can be asked this way; a question no fact type covers is answered by the setting's action and theme oracle, Ironsworn's "spark an idea" rather than a yes or no. A fact lasts until the player leaves the place, and a hit on an action that depended on it can settle it; neither rule has a source.

## From the design document

Places where Straightjacket departs from the design document's architectural recommendations or settles one of its open questions. Each was an explicit decision, taken on empirical grounds or as a deliberate scope boundary.

**Input parsing as a separate call.** The design document integrates input parsing into the single narrator call: the AI classifies the action and narrates the result in one response. Straightjacket classifies the input in a separate Brain call before the narrator writes. The split was already present in EdgeTales, where the integrated approach did not classify reliably, and Straightjacket, a fork of EdgeTales, kept it on the same grounds; because the two share a lineage (ORIGINS.md), this is one line of evidence carried forward, not two independent confirmations.

**Tool calling per data type, not as the universal mechanism.** The design document proposes tool calling as the central way the engine and the AI communicate. Straightjacket treats tool calling and prompt injection as complementary and picks per data type (`docs/ai.md`). The most visible consequence is that the Brain gets all game state through its prompt, because it must not choose which moves are available or which NPCs are present. The core principle, that tools determine results and the AI narrates, holds for every role.

**Narration and extraction as two calls.** The design document treats narration as one AI call per turn. Straightjacket runs a second call after the prose is written, in which an extraction role reads the narration and returns structured NPC data for the engine; opening setup, the revelation check, the recap, and the chapter summary are separate calls too. Asking the narrator to emit structured metadata in the same response degraded the prose; the extraction call does not have to balance two output contracts.

**No narration validator.** The design document leaves constraint verification open, with three candidates: a second, cheaper AI call that checks the output; severity markers the engine checks programmatically; or leaning harder on engine-dictated consequences so there is less to verify. Straightjacket built the first as a validator (an AI pass, a regex pass, and a retry loop), ran it for many versions, and removed it in 2026.04.27.8 and .9, together with the validators for the architect and the chapter summary. Judging writing rules with an AI proved unreliable on rules that are ambiguous even for people, and the retry loop flattened the prose as the narrator was steered away from anything the validator might flag. The principle now is the third option: the engine narrows what the AI can produce (dictated consequences, oracle-driven generation, computed pacing, vocabulary control) instead of checking what it produced, and no post-hoc validators or retry loops that judge AI output against writing rules are built. A diagnostic measurement without retry and without prompt injection remains an option once the roadmap is complete.

**The relationship layer is scoped to schemes and individual relationships.** The design document describes faction schemes with goal clocks, faction-level reputation, NPC loyalty thresholds, and a dynamic scale of emotional requests, refusals, and concessions, without settling whether that scale also runs between factions. In scope for Straightjacket: the individual scale (emotional requests, refusals, concessions, and relationship events between an NPC and the player), NPC-NPC triangles that involve the player, and factions that act through schemes with their own goal clocks, reputation with the player, and scheme interactions that surface as consequences. Out of scope, as a boundary rather than a deferral: emotional dynamics between factions as a layer of their own. None of this layer is built yet; today NPCs act individually through their agendas and the engine's agency checks, and react to their standing disposition and bond rather than to the history of the relationship (roadmap steps 14 to 24).
