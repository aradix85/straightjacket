# Deliberate divergences

The register of every place where the engine departs on purpose from a rulebook it draws on, or from the architecture the [Narrative RPG Engine](https://blindgamer85.itch.io/narrative-rpg-engine-accessible-solo-tabletop-with-ai-as-narrator-and-systems-u) design document proposes. A rule taken from a rulebook that is not listed here follows its source. When to add, change, or remove an entry, and which differences need none, is in CONTRIBUTING.md (Record every departure from a source); a project-rule scan rejects an entry without a status or with a roadmap step that does not exist.

Departures are judged by the order of preference in ARCHITECTURE.md: moving a decision from the AI to a system needs little argument, moving one toward the AI needs a measured one.

## From the source rulebooks

`tests/test_rules_conformance.py` pins the rules checked against Ironsworn/Starforged, Mythic GME 2e, the Adventure Crafter, and Blades in the Dark, including an automated comparison of every unconditional momentum gain in the Datasworn move texts with `engine/move_outcomes.yaml`.

- **Chaos factor per turn.** Mythic adjusts the chaos factor once per scene, by whether the player characters were in control. The engine treats each turn as a scene and reads control from the roll: a miss raises it by one, a strong hit lowers it by one, a weak hit leaves it, and dialog moves it by the NPC's disposition (`engine/chaos.yaml`); start 5 and range 1 to 9 follow Mythic. Status: Permanent, because reading control from the roll keeps an AI judgment out of the chaos factor.
- **Classic Ironsworn borrows one Starforged impact rule.** Wounded or shaken blocks recovery in every setting, where classic asks only for health or spirit above 0. Status: Until step 9j.
- **Succession in classic Ironsworn.** Continue a Legacy is a Starforged move; the engine offers succession in every setting. A classic successor inherits the world, its threats, and its NPCs, but no legacy boxes, since classic earns experience by its own rule. Status: Permanent, because a campaign that outlives its character is part of the engine in every setting, and classic has no rule of its own for it.
- **Make a Connection shifts the NPC's disposition** on a strong hit, with no rulebook number behind it. Status: Permanent, because the disposition feeds the stance and information gate, which have no rulebook counterpart.
- **Fixed choices where the player would choose.** Where an outcome lets the player pick a cost (Take Decisive Action and End the Fight on a weak hit, classic Reach Your Destination on a miss), the engine pays the price instead, and where it lets the player pick a benefit, the engine takes a fixed one: on Enter the Fray's weak hit the momentum, so the foe is in control, and on a hit on classic Sojourn the Clear a Condition option for the oldest marked condition, beside the fixed gains. Status: Permanent, because a choice menu breaks the text-only flow, and the engine, not the AI, still decides.
- **Suffering takes fixed choices.** Where harm, stress, and the thresholds let the player choose, the engine takes one choice (`docs/mechanics.md`, Harm, stress, and death): in Starforged and Sundered Isles it never chooses to resist harm or stress while the meter is above 0; a strong hit on Endure shakes it off when it can; a miss at 0 marks the first unmarked impact the move offers and rolls the move's table only when both are marked; the table's give-up result forsakes the newest active vow, and its unconscious result faces death only while a combat track is open; a weak hit on Face Death or Face Desolation returns bound to an extreme vow, where classic also allows formidable, and never takes the noble sacrifice; supply lost while unprepared costs momentum, where classic also allows health or spirit; and a table result that asks for a later roll (reeling) is told, not tracked. Status: Permanent, because a choice menu breaks the text-only flow, and the engine, not the AI, still decides.
- **Dying lasts two turns.** Endure Harm's dying result gives an hour or two to Heal and raise health above 0, or Face Death; the engine counts two turns on a clock (`engine/suffer.yaml` → `dying_clock`) and then makes Face Death. Status: Permanent, the user's choice, because the engine counts time in turns.
- **Momentum at its minimum absorbs further loss.** At -6 momentum, classic's Face a Setback and Starforged's Lose Momentum send the extra loss to another meter or to the progress of a track; the engine drops it. Status: Until step 13b.
- **Pay the Price costs 1.** The engine rolls the setting's Datasworn table (`engine/pay_the_price.yaml`); a row that harms, stresses, or wastes resources costs 1 health, spirit, or supply, where the rules leave the size to the player. Status: Permanent, the smallest amount the rules allow.
- **A miss withholds by engine rule.** The rulebooks give a miss's meaning and leave the telling to the player; the engine decides what the narrator has on a miss (`docs/mechanics.md`, What a miss withholds), because instructions alone did not stop clues leaking. Measured on audited misses: result integrity 3.0 of 5 before, 3.4 after. Status: Permanent, while the measurement holds.
- **A chained move is rolled at once.** Where an outcome says to make another move, the engine rolls it in the same turn; a chained move without a roll is an oracle move (`oracle_moves` in `engine/move_outcomes.yaml`) whose legacy ticks are marked at once, where the rules mark them when the discovery is first engaged, and Confront Chaos takes one aspect where the player may choose up to three. Status: Permanent, because the engine resolves a turn in one pass.
- **Roll bonuses are chosen for the player, and only adds are modelled.** The Brain decides whether an action meets a bonus's condition, an asset upgrade enables its abilities in order, and rerolls and other ability effects are not modelled (`docs/mechanics.md`, Impacts, legacy, and assets). Status: Until step 18.
- **A duel does not open a fight track.** Draw the Circle's boasts are modelled, but the duel opens no combat track, so the foe's initiative is narrated, not tracked. Status: Until step 13b.
- **Starforged's session moves are not moves.** Begin a Session, Set a Flag, Change Your Fate, Take a Break, and End a Session manage a table; Set a Flag's content lines are asked at creation and reach every prompt as content boundaries. Status: Permanent, because a solo game in a browser has no table to manage.
- **Mythic's rules for running Mythic as the game system are not used.** Its NPC statistics and fate questions as rules resolutions are replaced by Ironsworn's moves, ranks, and stats. Status: Permanent, because the Ironsworn rules already cover what these parts decide.
- **A fate-chart roll of 100 counts as doubles** and triggers a random event. Status: Permanent, because Mythic 2e does not settle this case.
- **Position and effect are computed by the engine.** In Blades in the Dark the game master sets them, and position decides how severe consequences are. The engine computes both from the game state (`docs/mechanics.md`, Position and effect); position decides how far a miss or a weak hit ticks a threat clock, and effect only reaches the narrator. Status: Permanent, because the engine, not the AI, sets the stakes.
- **Marked conditions block only Endure and the recovery moves.** In Starforged and Sundered Isles a marked wounded, shaken, or unprepared keeps its meter from rising except through the move that clears it; the engine checks the condition only in Endure Harm, Endure Stress, Heal, Hearten, and Resupply, so Sojourn's fixed gains raise the meter even while its condition is marked. Status: Until step 9j.
- **Threats advance by engine rule, not by Advance a Threat.** Delve rolls Advance a Threat when the character gives ground to a threat through inaction, failure, or delay: its table marks menace once or twice or offers a chance to prevent the development, and a match exposes a surprising aspect of the threat. The engine marks one menace on every miss for each active threat linked to an active vow, one when a random event aims at a threat, and gives every active threat a chance each turn to mark one on its own (`engine/threats.yaml`); the table is not rolled. Status: Until step 9j.
- **Forsake Your Vow costs a fixed amount of spirit.** A full menace track (Delve) and Endure Stress's give-up result send the character to Forsake Your Vow, which in classic Ironsworn costs spirit equal to the vow's rank through Endure Stress and in Starforged lets the player choose the costs. The engine fails the vow and takes `suffer.forsake_vow.cost` spirit in every setting, as harm that goes through Endure Stress where the setting's rule says so (`mechanics/tracks.py` → `forsake_vow`). Status: Until step 9j.
- **A weak-hit complication can become a clock that fills on its own.** Gather Information and Compel only name the complication; following Blades in the Dark, which turns a complication into a clock, the engine may start a small threat clock for it that fills every scene and strikes when full, and a hit the Brain aims at it removes it (`docs/mechanics.md`, Complication clocks). Blades leaves ticking and clearing to the game master. Status: Permanent, the user's choice, because the engine, not the AI, then keeps the danger's pace.
- **Yes/no questions go through Mythic's fate chart, with odds the engine derives.** Ironsworn's Ask the Oracle and Mythic's fate question let the player choose the odds; the engine derives them from the fact type and the game state (`engine/fact_resolution.yaml`) and uses one odds scale, and a question no fact type covers gets the setting's action and theme oracle. A fact lasts until the player leaves the place, and a hit on an action that depended on a yes can settle it; neither rule has a source. Status: Permanent, because the engine, not the player, decides what the fiction holds.
- **The engine reads which threads a scene invoked.** Mythic adds a thread to the list again each time it comes up in a scene, at the player's judgment. The engine counts a thread invoked when a random event aims at it, a rolled move acts on its vow, or a threat linked to its vow advances in the scene or is aimed at by a random event (`game/scene_finalization.py` → `invoked_threads`); a thread that only comes up in conversation gains nothing. Status: Permanent, because the engine, not the AI, reads which thread a scene invoked.
- **Meta plot points have no effect.** The Adventure Crafter sends a Meta result to the Meta Plot Points table, whose results change the Characters and Plotlines lists. The engine rolls a Meta result into a turning point as a plot point that does nothing; its handlers (`mechanics/adventure_crafter.py` → `dispatch_meta`) are not called. Status: Until step 9j.

## From the design document

- **Input parsing as a separate call.** The document classifies the action in the narrator's call; the engine classifies it in a separate Brain call first, because the engine must know the move before it rolls and the integrated approach did not classify reliably (in EdgeTales, before the fork). Status: Permanent.
- **Tool calling per data type.** The document makes tool calling the central link between engine and AI; the engine picks tool calling or prompt injection per data type (`docs/ai.md`, Tool calling and prompt injection), because the Brain must not choose which moves are available or which NPCs are present. Tools still determine results. Status: Permanent.
- **Narration and extraction as two calls.** The document has one AI call per turn; the engine extracts NPC data from the finished prose in a second call, because asking the narrator for structured metadata in the same answer degraded the prose. Status: Permanent.
- **No narration validator.** Of the document's options for verifying constraints, the engine leans on engine-dictated content and narrows what the AI can produce. An AI validator with a retry loop was built and removed: it judged writing rules unreliably, and its retries flattened the prose. Status: Permanent.
- **A failed AI call pauses instead of falling back to a simpler prompt.** The engine retries transient errors and then restores the state before the turn (`docs/ai.md`, When an AI call fails), because a simpler prompt would narrate without the constraints the turn needs. Status: Permanent.
- **WRONG and RIGHT examples in the narrator prompt.** The document asks for direction rather than exclusions; the narrator prompt pairs WRONG with RIGHT examples, because on GLM 5.3 the pairs raised judged player agency from 2.75 to 4.08 of 5, and a variant without them measured no better. Status: Permanent, while the measurement holds; any later change to the narrator prompt measures it again.
- **Foes and new tracks are decided by the AI where an oracle exists.** Every setting has a combat-action oracle (Delve a combat-event oracle) and classic a challenge-rank oracle; today the narrator invents the foe's action and the Brain picks a new track's rank. Status: Until step 13c.
- **The narrator invents what an NPC does off screen.** The document has NPC goal clocks fill and the system decide the action; today `mechanics/consequences.py` → `check_npc_agency` only tells the narrator that an NPC pursues its agenda. Status: Until step 12.
- **Places are rolled without the world state, and their names are narrated.** The document's generator layer rolls structured content consistent with the world state, which the AI then describes. The engine rolls a place from the setting's tables when the character first arrives (`docs/mechanics.md`, Generators), but nothing weights those tables by the world state, and the place's name is the Brain's or the narrator's. Status: Until step 10.
- **Encounters are narrated, not generated.** The document's encounter generator selects an event weighted by the area; today, apart from Mythic's random events, the narrator invents what the character runs into. Status: Until step 10.
- **Who a new NPC is, is narrated and then extracted.** The document's generator creates an NPC from tables; today only the name is rolled; the narrator invents look, role, and manner, and the metadata extraction picks the NPC's disposition from the engine's list. Status: Until step 11.
- **An AI call judges whether a revelation was told.** This register rules out post-hoc validators of AI output, yet `ai/brain.py` → `call_revelation_check` asks an AI whether the narration contained a planned revelation before marking it revealed. Status: Until step 9i.
- **The relationship layer is scoped to schemes and individual relationships.** The document leaves open whether its scale of emotional requests, refusals, and concessions also runs between factions. In scope: that scale between an NPC and the player, NPC-NPC triangles involving the player, and factions acting through schemes with goal clocks and reputation with the player; emotional dynamics between factions are out of scope. None of it is built yet. Status: Permanent, for the boundary; roadmap steps 14 to 24 build the layer.
- **Mythic and the Adventure Crafter are used directly.** The document assumed Mythic was not openly licensed and that a builder would write equivalent tables; both are now CC BY-NC 4.0, so the engine uses their tables as data, which makes redistribution non-commercial (README, License). Status: Permanent.
