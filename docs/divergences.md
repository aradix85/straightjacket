# Deliberate divergences

Where Straightjacket departs on purpose from the rulebooks it draws on, or from the architecture the design document proposes ([Narrative RPG Engine](https://blindgamer85.itch.io/narrative-rpg-engine-accessible-solo-tabletop-with-ai-as-narrator-and-systems-u), published on itch.io). A rule taken from a rulebook that is not listed here follows its source. The design document is a theoretical concept and this engine its practical implementation, so details differ wherever building it called for it; the register records the departures from the document's architectural recommendations and the open questions it settles, not every difference in detail.

When a source and practice disagree, the order of preference decides (ARCHITECTURE.md, The core idea): a tabletop system decides first, an engine rule second, and the AI only writes prose and reads the player's free text. A departure that moves a decision from the AI to a system needs little argument; one that moves a decision toward the AI needs a measured one.

Every entry names what the source says, what the engine does instead, and why, and ends with a status: Permanent (the departure stays, and the entry says why, with the measurement or decision behind it), Until step followed by a roadmap step (that step brings the engine in line with the source, and its commit removes the entry), or Open (nobody has decided yet whether the departure stays; the roadmap lists the decision).

A change that departs from a source adds its entry in the same commit, and a project-rule scan rejects an entry without a status or with a step the roadmap does not have.

## From the source rulebooks

A rule taken from Ironsworn/Starforged, Mythic GME 2e, the Adventure Crafter, or Blades in the Dark works as its source describes, or it is listed below. `tests/test_rules_conformance.py` pins the checked rules, including an automated comparison of every unconditional momentum gain in the Datasworn move texts with `engine/move_outcomes.yaml`.

- **Chaos factor per turn.** Mythic adjusts the chaos factor once per scene, by whether the player characters were in control. Straightjacket treats each turn as a scene and reads control from the roll: a miss raises the chaos factor by one, a strong hit lowers it by one, a weak hit leaves it, and dialog moves it by the NPC's disposition (`engine/chaos.yaml`). Start 5 and range 1 to 9 follow Mythic. Status: Permanent, because a turn is the engine's scene and reading control from the roll keeps an AI judgment out of the chaos factor.
- **Classic Ironsworn borrows one Starforged impact rule.** `engine/move_outcomes.yaml` follows Starforged, and its `move_outcome_overrides` hold the classic differences (Secure an Advantage, Endure Harm, Endure Stress); classic marks its own lasting harm (`maimed`, `corrupted`, `encumbered` in `engine/impacts.yaml`). What classic still borrows is that wounded or shaken blocks recovery, where Ironsworn only asks for health or spirit above 0. Status: Until step 9j.
- **Succession in classic Ironsworn.** Continue a Legacy is a Starforged move; Straightjacket offers succession in every setting. Classic earns experience by its own rule when a vow is fulfilled and no longer fills Starforged's legacy tracks, so a classic successor inherits the world, its threats, and its NPCs, but no legacy boxes. Status: Permanent, because a campaign that outlives its character is part of the engine in every setting, and classic has no rule of its own for it.
- **Make a Connection shifts the NPC's disposition** on a strong hit, an engine addition with no rulebook number behind it; the move itself gives no momentum and no progress. Status: Permanent, because the disposition feeds the stance and information gate, which have no rulebook counterpart.
- **Fixed choices where the player would choose a cost.** Where an outcome lets the player pick a cost from a list (Take Decisive Action and End the Fight on a weak hit, classic Reach Your Destination on a miss), the engine pays the price instead. Status: Permanent, because a choice menu breaks the text-only flow and the engine, not the AI, still decides.
- **Pay the Price rolls the official table and costs 1.** `engine/pay_the_price.yaml` names the setting's Datasworn table; a row that says the character is harmed, stressed, or wastes resources costs 1 health, spirit, or supply, the smallest suffer amount, where the rules leave the size to the player. Status: Permanent, the smallest amount the rules allow.
- **A miss withholds by engine rule.** The rulebooks give a miss's meaning (Gather Information: a dire threat or unwelcome truth; Compel: a refusal) and leave the telling to the player. The engine decides what the narrator has on a miss (docs/mechanics.md, What a miss withholds), because instructions did not stop clues leaking; these specifics are the engine's reading, not the rulebooks'. Measured in step 9c: result integrity on audited misses 3.0 of 5 before, 3.4 after. Status: Permanent, while the measurements hold.
- **A chained move is rolled at once, and only if it has a roll.** Where an outcome says to make another move (Test Your Relationship's hits say Develop Your Relationship), the effect `chain_move` makes `game/finalization.py` roll that move in the same turn, with the best stat its roll options allow or, for a connection move, the connection's rank, spending a banked next-move bonus; its consequences join the first move's for the narrator. A chained move without a roll is an oracle move configured under `oracle_moves` in `engine/move_outcomes.yaml`: the engine rolls its table and marks its legacy ticks at once, where the rules mark them when the discovery or aspect is first engaged; Confront Chaos lets the player choose one to three aspects, the engine takes one. Status: Permanent, because the engine resolves a turn in one pass.
- **Roll bonuses are chosen for the player, and only adds are modelled.** The Brain decides whether an action meets a bonus's condition and names at most one bonus per roll; an asset upgrade enables its abilities in order, where the player would choose (the mechanism is in `docs/mechanics.md`). Rerolls, other ability effects, and choosing the ability an upgrade enables come later. Status: Until step 18.
- **A duel does not open a fight track.** Draw the Circle starts a formal duel, and a miss or the boast Grant first strike gives the foe initiative; the engine models the boasts' momentum and costs but opens no combat track for the duel, so initiative is narrated, not tracked. Status: Until step 13b.
- **Starforged's session moves are not moves.** Begin a Session, Set a Flag, Change Your Fate, Take a Break, and End a Session are table practices; Set a Flag's content lines are asked at creation and reach every prompt as content boundaries, and the others have no game state to act on (decision 2026.04.28.3). Status: Permanent, because a solo game in a browser has no table to manage.
- **Mythic's rules for running Mythic as the game system are not used.** Mythic 2e's NPC statistics and its use of fate questions as RPG rules resolve actions and values when Mythic is the rules system; here Ironsworn's moves, ranks, and stats resolve them. Status: Permanent, because the Ironsworn rules already cover what these parts decide.
- **A fate-chart roll of 100 counts as doubles** and triggers a random event; Mythic 2e does not settle this edge case. Status: Permanent, the source is silent.
- **Position and effect are computed by the engine.** In Blades in the Dark the game master sets position and effect before a roll, and position decides how severe the consequences are. Straightjacket computes both from the game state (`docs/mechanics.md`, Position and effect); position decides how far a miss or a weak hit ticks a threat clock, Ironsworn's moves decide the rest of the outcome, and effect only reaches the narrator. Status: Permanent, because the engine, not the AI, sets the stakes, as the order of preference asks.
- **Game over when health and spirit are both 0.** Ironsworn and Starforged bring a character at 0 health or 0 spirit to Face Death or Face Desolation through Endure Harm and Endure Stress and their tables; no rule ends the game because both meters stand at 0. The engine ends it when both are 0 after a turn and marks a crisis when one of them is (`game/finalization.py` → `_update_crisis`). Status: Open; roadmap step 9j lists the decision.
- **Yes/no questions go through Mythic's fate chart, with odds the engine derives.** Ironsworn's Ask the Oracle and Mythic's fate question both let the player choose the odds. Straightjacket answers the facts an action or question depends on through Mythic's fate chart, so the chaos factor weighs in as Mythic intends and there is one odds scale, and derives the odds from the fact type and the game state (`engine/fact_resolution.yaml`); a question no fact type covers gets the setting's action and theme oracle, Ironsworn's "spark an idea". A fact lasts until the player leaves the place, and a hit on an action that depended on a yes can settle it; neither rule has a source. Status: Permanent, because the engine, not the player, decides what the fiction holds.

## From the design document

Places where Straightjacket departs from the design document's architectural recommendations or settles one of its open questions.

**Input parsing as a separate call.** The design document integrates input parsing into the single narrator call: the AI classifies the action and narrates the result in one response. Straightjacket classifies the input in a separate Brain call before the narrator writes. The split was already present in EdgeTales, where the integrated approach did not classify reliably, and Straightjacket, a fork of EdgeTales, kept it on the same grounds; because the two share a lineage (ORIGINS.md), this is one line of evidence carried forward, not two independent confirmations. Status: Permanent, because the engine must know the move before it rolls.

**Tool calling per data type, not as the universal mechanism.** The design document proposes tool calling as the central way the engine and the AI communicate. Straightjacket picks tool calling or prompt injection per data type (`docs/ai.md`); the Brain gets all game state through its prompt, because it must not choose which moves are available or which NPCs are present. The core principle, that tools determine results and the AI narrates, holds for every role. Status: Permanent.

**Narration and extraction as two calls.** The design document treats narration as one AI call per turn. Straightjacket runs a second call after the prose is written, in which an extraction role reads the narration and returns structured NPC data; opening setup, the revelation check, the recap, and blueprint voicing are separate calls too. Asking the narrator to emit structured metadata in the same response degraded the prose. Status: Permanent.

**No narration validator.** The design document leaves constraint verification open, with three candidates: a second AI call that checks the output, severity markers checked programmatically, or leaning harder on engine-dictated consequences. Straightjacket built the first as a validator with a retry loop, ran it for many versions, and removed it in 2026.04.27.8 and .9: judging writing rules with an AI proved unreliable, and the retry loop flattened the prose. The engine narrows what the AI can produce instead of checking what it produced. Status: Permanent.

**A failed AI call pauses instead of falling back to a simpler prompt.** The design document lets the engine retry or fall back to a simpler prompt. Straightjacket retries transient errors and then pauses the turn, restoring the state before it, because a simpler prompt would narrate without the constraints the turn needs (decision 2026.09.24.47). Status: Permanent.

**WRONG and RIGHT examples in the narrator prompt.** The design document's constraint principles ask for direction rather than exclusions. The narrator prompt pairs WRONG with RIGHT examples for player agency, genre physics, and NPC speech, because measured on GLM 5.3 the pairs raised judged player agency from 2.75 to 4.08 (2026.09.26.12), while a variant without its MUSTs and NEVERs was not adopted (2026.09.26.18). Status: Permanent, while the measurements hold; every narrator-prompt round (step 9f) measures it again.

**Foes and new tracks are decided by the AI where an oracle exists.** Every setting carries a combat-action oracle (Delve a combat-event oracle) for what a foe does, and classic a challenge-rank oracle for how hard a new vow or foe is. Today the narrator invents the foe's action, and the Brain picks a new track's rank. Status: Until step 13c.

**The narrator still invents what an NPC does off screen.** The design document has NPC goal clocks fill and then the NPC act, with the action decided by the system. Today `mechanics/consequences.py` → `check_npc_agency` tells the narrator every few scenes that an NPC pursues its agenda, and the narrator invents the concrete action. Status: Until step 12.

**Locations and settlements are narrated, not generated.** The design document's generator layer rolls structured content that the AI then describes, consistent with the world state. Today only NPC names, threats, clocks, and facts come from the engine; the narrator invents the places. Status: Until step 9d.

**Encounters are narrated, not generated.** The design document's encounter generator selects an event weighted to the properties of the area. Today, apart from Mythic's random events, the narrator invents what the character runs into. Status: Until step 10.

**Who a new NPC is, is narrated and then extracted.** The design document's generator creates an NPC from tables before the AI describes it. Today only the name is rolled; the narrator invents look, role, and manner, and the metadata extraction registers them. Status: Until step 11.

**An AI call judges whether a revelation was told.** The design document leaves verification to engine-dictated content, and this register rules out post-hoc validators of AI output. Yet `ai/brain.py` → `call_revelation_check` asks an AI whether the narration contained a planned revelation, and only then marks it revealed. Status: Until step 9i.

**The relationship layer is scoped to schemes and individual relationships.** The design document describes faction schemes with goal clocks, faction-level reputation, NPC loyalty thresholds, and a dynamic scale of emotional requests, refusals, and concessions, without settling whether that scale also runs between factions. In scope: the individual scale between an NPC and the player, NPC-NPC triangles that involve the player, and factions that act through schemes with their own goal clocks, reputation with the player, and scheme interactions that surface as consequences. Out of scope, as a boundary rather than a deferral: emotional dynamics between factions as a layer of their own. None of this layer is built yet (roadmap steps 14 to 24). Status: Permanent, for the boundary; the individual and faction scales come in steps 14 to 24.

**Mythic and the Adventure Crafter are used directly.** The design document assumed Mythic GME was not openly licensed and that a builder would write equivalent meaning tables. Mythic GME 2e and the Adventure Crafter are now available under CC BY-NC 4.0, so Straightjacket uses their tables as data (README, License), which makes redistribution non-commercial. Status: Permanent.
