# Deliberate divergences

The register of every place where the engine departs on purpose from a rulebook it draws on, or from the architecture the [Narrative RPG Engine](https://blindgamer85.itch.io/narrative-rpg-engine-accessible-solo-tabletop-with-ai-as-narrator-and-systems-u) design document proposes. A rule taken from a rulebook that is not listed here follows its source. When to add, change, or remove an entry, and which differences need none, is in CONTRIBUTING.md (Record every departure from a source); a project-rule scan rejects an entry without a status or with a roadmap step that does not exist.

Departures are judged by the order of preference in ARCHITECTURE.md: moving a decision from the AI to a system needs little argument, moving one toward the AI needs a measured one.

## From the source rulebooks

`tests/test_rules_conformance.py` pins the rules checked against Ironsworn/Starforged, Mythic GME 2e, the Adventure Crafter, and Blades in the Dark, including an automated comparison of every unconditional momentum gain in the classic and Starforged move texts with `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`.

- **Chaos factor per turn.** Mythic adjusts the chaos factor once per scene, by whether the player characters were in control. The engine treats each turn as a scene and reads control from the roll: a miss raises it by one, a strong hit lowers it by one, a weak hit leaves it, and dialog moves it by the NPC's disposition (`gamemaster/chaos.yaml`); the range of 1 to 9 follows Mythic, and so does the start of 5 for a chapter and a successor, while a new game's start moves by up to two with words of the background vow (`ruleset/creation.yaml` → `chaos_vow_modifiers`), which has no source and goes in step 9t. Status: Permanent, because reading control from the roll keeps an AI judgment out of the chaos factor.
- **Succession in classic Ironsworn.** Continue a Legacy is a Starforged move; the engine offers succession in every setting. A classic successor inherits the world, its threats, and its NPCs, and a share of classic's bonds legacy track, which Forge a Bond marks, as a Starforged successor does. Status: Permanent, because a campaign that outlives its character is part of the engine in every setting, and classic has no rule of its own for it.
- **Make a Connection shifts the NPC's disposition** on a strong hit, with no rulebook number behind it, and so does Test Your Relationship, a rule EdgeTales brought that step 9t reads against the book. Status: Permanent, because the disposition feeds the stance and information gate, which have no rulebook counterpart.
- **Starforged's recover and connection moves take fixed costs.** Resupply's weak hit costs 1 momentum where the book asks for a cost or complication first, Sojourn takes fixed gains where it makes recover moves, and Test Your Relationship's miss pays the price and keeps the connection, where the book loses it or lets the player prove their loyalty. Status: Until step 9t.
- **Fixed choices where the player would choose.** Where an outcome lets the player pick a cost (End the Fight on a weak hit), the engine pays the price instead; where it lets the player roll on the move's table or pick from it (Take Decisive Action's weak hit) or envision with a table's help (Story Clue on a hit on Gather Information), the engine rolls; and where it lets the player pick a benefit, the engine takes a fixed one: on Enter the Fray's weak hit the momentum, so the foe is in control, and on a hit on classic Sojourn the Clear a Condition option for the oldest marked condition, beside the fixed gains. On a miss on Fulfill Your Vow, Forge a Bond, Finish an Expedition, or Reach Your Destination, where the player may give up or go on, the engine recommits (the user's choice); on Forge a Bond's weak hit, in both rulesets, it seals the bond at once and tells what they ask first, where the book seals it once the request is met (the user's choice), and classic Forge a Bond's strong hit takes the spirit; on a hit on Forge a Bond it bolsters the connection's influence, so they aid with +2, where the player may instead give them a second role, which the engine does not track; and on Fulfill Your Vow's weak hit it swears no vow to set things right, so the reward is one rank lower. The other choices go the same way: Secure an Advantage takes the momentum (Starforged's weak hit, classic's strong hit), Check Your Gear's weak hit and classic Heal's weak hit sacrifice supply, Starforged Heal's weak hit loses momentum, Resupply's strong hit takes supply rather than an item, classic Make Camp takes health and momentum, classic Resupply's weak hit takes the full +2 supply at -2 momentum, classic Test Your Bond's strong hit takes spirit, Gain Ground takes progress and momentum, Explore a Waypoint's strong hit takes the momentum, the weak hits of Undertake an Expedition and Set a Course take the suffer costs, and classic Undertake a Journey's and Reach Your Destination's strong hits take one option each (`ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`). Where a source only suggests, the engine always does it: a miss with a match rolls Story Complication, an altered scene always rolls Mythic's Scene Adjustment Table, an Ambiguous Event always rolls the Descriptions table, and a Starforged NPC takes its callsign as its name half the time (`naming.callsign_probability`). Status: Permanent, because a choice menu breaks the text-only flow, and the engine, not the AI, still decides.
- **Suffering takes fixed choices.** Where harm, stress, and the thresholds let the player choose, the engine takes one choice (`docs/mechanics.md`, Harm, stress, and death): in Starforged and Sundered Isles it never chooses to resist harm or stress while the meter is above 0; a strong hit on Endure shakes it off when it can; a miss at 0 marks the first unmarked impact the move offers and rolls the move's table only when both are marked; the table's give-up result forsakes the newest active vow, and its unconscious result faces death only while a combat track is open; a weak hit on Face Death or Face Desolation returns bound to an extreme vow, where classic also allows formidable, and never takes the noble sacrifice; supply lost while unprepared costs momentum, where classic also allows health or spirit; a table result that asks for a later roll (reeling) is told, not tracked; a "make a suffer move" takes its cost from the highest condition meter, never from momentum; a miss on Endure takes the extra loss from the meter before momentum; and Starforged's weak-hit exchange of momentum for health or spirit is taken whenever it can be. Status: Permanent, because a choice menu breaks the text-only flow, and the engine, not the AI, still decides.
- **Dying lasts two turns.** Endure Harm's dying result gives an hour or two to Heal and raise health above 0, or Face Death; the engine counts two turns on a clock (`ruleset/suffer.yaml` → `dying_clock`) and then makes Face Death. Status: Permanent, the user's choice, because the engine counts time in turns.
- **Momentum at its minimum absorbs further loss.** At -6 momentum, classic's Face a Setback and Starforged's Lose Momentum send the extra loss to another meter or to the progress of a track; the engine drops it. Status: Until step 13b.
- **Pay the Price costs 1.** The engine rolls the move's own Datasworn table (`ruleset/pay_the_price.yaml`); a row that harms, stresses, or wastes resources costs 1 health, spirit, or supply, where the rules leave the size to the player. Status: Permanent, the smallest amount the rules allow.
- **A miss withholds by engine rule.** The rulebooks give a miss's meaning and leave the telling to the player; the engine decides what the narrator has on a miss (`docs/mechanics.md`, What a miss withholds), because instructions alone did not stop clues leaking. Measured on audited misses: result integrity 3.0 of 5 before, 3.4 after. On a hit it goes the other way: a target whose stance refuses yields (`gamemaster/information_gate.yaml` → `hit_stance`), as the moves' hits say. Status: Permanent, while the measurement holds.
- **A classic bond is full closeness.** Classic Ironsworn knows a bond with a person, not a degree of closeness; the engine counts a sealed bond as 10 of 10, the closeness a full Starforged connection gives, and an NPC without one as 0 (the user's choice). Status: Permanent, because closeness feeds the stance, the information gate, position, and keyed scenes, which have no classic counterpart.
- **Develop Your Relationship without a bond comes only from Test Your Relationship.** Without a bond the move marks progress and rolls nothing, and the engine has no turn for a move without a roll yet, so it makes this branch only as the follow-up of a hit on Test Your Relationship; the player's own Develop Your Relationship is offered only with a bond. Status: Until step 13b.
- **A chained move is rolled at once.** Where an outcome says to make another move, the engine rolls it in the same turn; a chained move without a roll is an oracle move (`oracle_moves` in `ruleset/move_outcomes.yaml`) whose legacy ticks are marked at once, where the rules mark them when the discovery is first engaged, and Confront Chaos takes one aspect where the player may choose up to three. Status: Permanent, because the engine resolves a turn in one pass.
- **Delve's site moves play without a site.** A missed Escape the Depths reveals a danger, and the engine rolls Reveal a Danger again on a row that sends to the site's theme or domain card (`reroll_prefixes` in `oracle_moves`), since no site exists to hold them; Escape the Depths, which Delve offers today, takes none of its weak hit's costs; and a miss on Locate Your Objective fails the track where the move lets the player go on, a move not offered before a site exists. Status: Until step 26.
- **Roll bonuses are chosen for the player, and only adds are modelled.** The Brain decides whether an action meets a bonus's condition, an asset upgrade enables its abilities in order, and rerolls and other ability effects are not modelled (`docs/mechanics.md`, Impacts, legacy, and assets). Status: Until step 18.
- **A duel does not open a fight track.** Draw the Circle's boasts are modelled, but the duel opens no combat track, so the foe's initiative is narrated, not tracked. Status: Until step 13b.
- **Starforged's session moves are not moves.** Begin a Session, Set a Flag, Change Your Fate, Take a Break, and End a Session manage a table; Set a Flag's content lines are asked at creation and reach every prompt as content boundaries. Status: Permanent, because a solo game in a browser has no table to manage.
- **Mythic's rules for running Mythic as the game system are not used.** Its NPC statistics and fate questions as rules resolutions are replaced by Ironsworn's moves, ranks, and stats. Status: Permanent, because the Ironsworn rules already cover what these parts decide.
- **A fate-chart roll of 100 counts as doubles** and triggers a random event. Status: Permanent, because Mythic 2e does not settle this case.
- **Position and effect are computed by the engine.** In Blades in the Dark the game master sets them, and position decides how severe consequences are. The engine computes both from the game state (`docs/mechanics.md`, Position and effect); position decides how far a miss or a weak hit ticks the first open threat clock, and effect only reaches the narrator. Status: Permanent, because the engine, not the AI, sets the stakes.
- **Threats advance by engine rule, not by Advance a Threat.** Delve rolls Advance a Threat when the character gives ground to a threat through inaction, failure, or delay: its table marks menace once or twice or offers a chance to prevent the development, and a match exposes a surprising aspect of the threat. The engine marks one menace on every miss for each active threat linked to an active vow, one when a random event aims at a threat, and gives every active threat a chance each turn to mark one on its own (`ruleset/threats.yaml`); the table is not rolled. Status: Until step 9j.
- **Forsake Your Vow costs a fixed amount of spirit.** A full menace track (Delve) and Endure Stress's give-up result send the character to Forsake Your Vow, which in classic Ironsworn costs spirit equal to the vow's rank through Endure Stress and in Starforged lets the player choose the costs. The engine fails the vow and takes `suffer.forsake_vow.cost` spirit in every setting, as harm that goes through Endure Stress where the setting's rule says so (`ruleset/tracks.py` → `forsake_vow`). Status: Until step 9j.
- **Threats meet their end by engine rule.** Delve's last menace box means the threat achieves its goal; a threat no vow is sworn against is resolved without an event when its menace fills (`ruleset/threats.py` → `resolve_full_menace`). A random event whose focus is PC Negative or Move Away From A Thread aims at an open threat half the time (`gamemaster/random_events.yaml` → `threat_target_probability`), and a vow fulfilled against a threat at half its menace or more gives 2 experience in every setting (`ruleset/legacy.yaml` → `threat_overcome_bonus`, `threat_overcome_threshold`), which Datasworn does not hold. Status: Until step 9j.
- **A weak-hit complication can become a clock that fills on its own.** Gather Information, Compel, and Make a Connection only name the complication; following Blades in the Dark, which turns a complication into a clock, the engine may start a small threat clock for it that fills every scene and strikes when full, and a hit the Brain aims at it removes it (`docs/mechanics.md`, Complication clocks). Blades leaves ticking and clearing to the game master. Status: Permanent, the user's choice, because the engine, not the AI, then keeps the danger's pace.
- **Yes/no questions go through Mythic's fate chart, with odds the engine derives.** Ironsworn's Ask the Oracle and Mythic's fate question let the player choose the odds; the engine derives them from the fact type and the game state (`gamemaster/fact_resolution.yaml`) and uses one odds scale, and a question no fact type covers gets the setting's action and theme oracle. A fact lasts until the player leaves the place, a hit on an action that depended on a yes can settle it, and a no on whether the action's target is present takes the target from the action (`no_releases_target` in `gamemaster/fact_resolution.yaml`); none of these rules has a source. Status: Permanent, because the engine, not the player, decides what the fiction holds.
- **The engine reads which threads a scene invoked.** Mythic adds a thread to the list again each time it comes up in a scene, at the player's judgment. The engine counts a thread invoked when a random event aims at it, a rolled move acts on its vow, or a threat linked to its vow advances in the scene or is aimed at by a random event (`game/scene_finalization.py` → `invoked_threads`); a thread that only comes up in conversation gains nothing. Status: Permanent, because the engine, not the AI, reads which thread a scene invoked.
- **Keyed scenes are the engine's.** Mythic leaves a keyed scene's trigger to the player, and an Adventure Crafter plot point plays in its own turning point. The engine keys 33 plot points to triggers, thresholds, and hints of its own (`crafters/adventure_crafter.yaml` → `keyed_scene_mapping`, at most `max_keyed_scenes_per_chapter` a chapter), keys an NPC Action, NPC Positive, or NPC Negative random event, and a PC Negative one aimed at a threat, to a later scene (`gamemaster/random_events.yaml` → `keyed_scene_mapping`), and gives the opening clock and every threat clock random events and plot points start keyed scenes at half and full, and a progress clock one at full (`gamemaster/clock_keyed_scenes.yaml`). Fifteen of the plot points wait for a bond, which classic Ironsworn and Delve raise only when Forge a Bond seals one, and then at once to full. Status: Until step 32b, which reads the plot points' keyed scenes against the books; the keyed scenes of events and clocks stay the engine's.
- **Random events and plot points start dangers and clocks.** Mythic resolves a random event at once, and Blades leaves making a clock to the game master. The engine makes a PC Negative or NPC Negative event a new threat, a PC Negative or Move Away event a threat clock and a Move Toward event a progress clock, five Adventure Crafter plot points a threat and four a threat clock, each clock of `clocks.default_segments` segments, while fewer of them are open than `threats.max_open_emergent` and `clocks.max_open_emergent` allow (`docs/mechanics.md`, Threats and clocks). Every world-owned threat clock also ticks on its own at a scene's end by `pacing.autonomous_clock_tick_chance`; a progress clock is ticked by nothing. Status: Until step 14b.
- **A New NPC event draws a known character.** Mythic's New NPC focus brings a new character into the adventure; the engine rolls the Characters List for it, as for the other NPC foci (`gamemaster/random_events.yaml` → `npc_focus_categories`). Status: Until step 9t.
- **The engine chooses the Adventure Crafter's plotline.** Choose Most Logical is the player's pick, and plotlines fill the list's lines; the engine takes the advancing plotline with the fewest turning points and writes no plotline onto a line, so New Plotline keeps its odds all chapter (`crafters/adventure_crafter.py` → `_select_active_plotline`). Its theme roll can give a theme twice, which step 9j checks against the book's ranking of the five themes. Status: Until step 9j.
- **A successor inherits by engine rule.** Starforged's Continue a Legacy rolls each legacy track for an option, such as an asset, a connection, or a quest to see through. The engine carries a share of each legacy track's boxes by the roll (`ruleset/succession.yaml` → `inheritance`), keeps NPCs with a share of their connection by their status (`npc_carryover`), drops the predecessor's vows and creation threads, clears every clock, and keeps every threat (`game/succession.py`). Status: Until step 9t.
- **Time of day is the engine's.** No rulebook tracks the time of day. The engine keeps eight phases (`gamemaster/enums.yaml` → `time_phases`), moves them by each move's time (`ruleset/time_progression_map.yaml`, `gamemaster/moves.yaml`, `gamemaster/time_progression_steps.yaml`) and by a change of place, as EdgeTales did, and starts every opening in the morning (`gamemaster/opening.yaml`). Status: Permanent, because no rulebook tracks the time of day.
- **A move's own time is not read.** A move whose text names its time, such as classic Make Camp's several hours, takes the short time most moves take, which moves no phase. Status: Until step 9t.
- **Revelations land by engine rule.** The Adventure Crafter has no revelations; the engine seeds one per act from its turning point and lands it when its keyed scene fires, at the latest at its act's end, on a turn that does not fail (`docs/mechanics.md`, Revelations). Status: Permanent, the user's choice (roadmap 9i).
- **Meta plot points have no effect.** The Adventure Crafter sends a Meta result to the Meta Plot Points table, whose results change the Characters and Plotlines lists. The engine rolls a Meta result into a turning point as a plot point that does nothing; its handlers (`crafters/meta_plot_points.py` → `dispatch_meta`) are not called. Status: Until step 9j.

## From the design document

- **Input parsing as a separate call.** The document classifies the action in the narrator's call; the engine classifies it in a separate Brain call first, because the engine must know the move before it rolls and the integrated approach did not classify reliably (in EdgeTales, before the fork). Status: Permanent.
- **Tool calling per data type.** The document makes tool calling the central link between engine and AI; the engine picks tool calling or prompt injection per data type (`docs/ai.md`, Tool calling and prompt injection), because the Brain must not choose which moves are available or which NPCs are present. Tools still determine results. Status: Permanent.
- **Narration and extraction as two calls.** The document has one AI call per turn; the engine extracts NPC data from the finished prose in a second call, because asking the narrator for structured metadata in the same answer degraded the prose. Status: Permanent.
- **No narration validator.** Of the document's options for verifying constraints, the engine leans on engine-dictated content and narrows what the AI can produce, because an AI validator with a retry loop judged writing rules unreliably and its retries flattened the prose. Status: Permanent.
- **A failed AI call pauses instead of falling back to a simpler prompt.** The engine retries transient errors and then restores the state before the turn (`docs/ai.md`, When an AI call fails), because a simpler prompt would narrate without the constraints the turn needs. Status: Permanent.
- **WRONG and RIGHT examples in the narrator prompt.** The document asks for direction rather than exclusions; the narrator prompt pairs WRONG with RIGHT examples, which it held before the measurements of prompt tuning began. No variant without them was measured; one that added a pair measured no better, and rewording the narrator on Sonnet 5.5 stayed within the noise (roadmap, 9f). Status: Permanent, until a measured variant without them does better.
- **Foes and new tracks are decided by the AI where an oracle exists.** Every setting has a combat-action oracle (Delve a combat-event oracle) and classic a challenge-rank oracle; today the narrator invents the foe's action and the Brain picks a new track's rank. Status: Until step 13c.
- **An AI Director steers the story and the NPCs.** The document has the AI decide neither what happens next nor what an NPC does or feels. The Director, an AI call that came with EdgeTales, writes guidance for the next scenes that the narrator follows (`narrator_guidance`), notes on what an NPC should do or feel (`npc_guidance`), and reflections that set how an NPC now sees the player, with its agenda, instinct, arc, and description (`prompts/director.yaml`). It runs every three scenes and on the triggers `docs/ai.md` lists, among them a reflection that falls due when EdgeTales' importance scores of an NPC's memories add up (`director.py` → `should_call_director`, `emotions/importance.yaml`, `npc.reflection_threshold`). Status: Until step 11, whose first substep decides with the user what the Director keeps.
- **The narrator gets less than the full relational context.** The document gives the AI an NPC's full relational context; the engine shows the target NPC by its information gate, from name and description at gate 0 to its secrets at gate 4, with a stance in place of its disposition and a budget of new facts (`gamemaster/information_gate.yaml`, `gamemaster/stance_matrix.yaml`, `gamemaster/stance_gate.py`). Status: Permanent, because the engine, not the AI, then decides how much of what an NPC knows reaches the narration.
- **Narrative direction reads the meters and the outcome.** The document derives intensity from threat levels, clock states, and stakes, tempo from the kind of scene, and the player's relation and the narrative position from what happened. The engine takes intensity from health and spirit, tempo and perspective from the outcome, sends the player's relation as always caused, and takes the narrative position from EdgeTales' run of intense and calm scenes, never as climax (`engine/narrative_direction.yaml`, `gamemaster/pacing.py` → `get_pacing_hint`). Status: Until step 14c.
- **The narration decides who dies.** An NPC dies when the narration shows it and the extraction reports it for an NPC who was in the scene (`ai/metadata.py` → `process_deceased_npcs_with_presence_check`); no rule decides it, in a fight or out of one. Status: Until step 13c, for foes in a fight.
- **The narrator invents what an NPC does off screen.** The document has NPC goal clocks fill and the system decide the action; today `gamemaster/clocks.py` → `check_npc_agency` only tells the narrator that an NPC pursues its agenda. Status: Until step 12.
- **Places are rolled without the world state, and their names are narrated.** The document's generator layer rolls structured content consistent with the world state, which the AI then describes. The engine rolls a place from the setting's tables when the character first arrives (`docs/mechanics.md`, Generators), but nothing weights those tables by the world state, and the place's name is the Brain's or the narrator's. Status: Until step 10.
- **Encounters are narrated, not generated.** The document's encounter generator selects an event weighted by the area; today, apart from Mythic's random events, the narrator invents what the character runs into. Status: Until step 10.
- **Who a new NPC is, is narrated and then extracted.** The document's generator creates an NPC from tables; today only the name is rolled; the narrator invents look, role, and manner, and the metadata extraction picks the NPC's disposition from the engine's list. Status: Until step 11.
- **The relationship layer is scoped to schemes and individual relationships.** The document leaves open whether its scale of emotional requests, refusals, and concessions also runs between factions. In scope: that scale between an NPC and the player, NPC-NPC triangles involving the player, and factions acting through schemes with goal clocks and reputation with the player; emotional dynamics between factions are out of scope. None of it is built yet. Status: Permanent, for the boundary; roadmap steps 14 to 24 build the layer.
- **Mythic and the Adventure Crafter are used directly.** The document assumed Mythic was not openly licensed and that a builder would write equivalent tables; both are CC BY-NC 4.0, so the engine uses their tables as data, which makes redistribution non-commercial (README, License). Status: Permanent.
